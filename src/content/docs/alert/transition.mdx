The handoff between phases is where investigations either gather momentum or stall. A clean Alert-to-Subject transition does three things:

## What Alert hands off, and what it only flags

One of Alert's three deliverables is finished work; the other two are honest lead lists. Alert does not classify assets, reconstruct attack chains, or score business impact; those verdicts belong to Subject, Scope, Uncover, and Risk. What Alert owes them is the validated alert plus every flag worth carrying forward, so no later phase starts from a cold read.

<div class="deliverables-grid">
  <div class="deliverable-card">
    <h3>🔧 Finished: the validated alert</h3>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>Detection mechanism analysis</strong>, what fired, by what logic, with what confidence per the <a href="/docs/alert/detection-mechanisms/#trust-calibration">trust-calibration table</a>.</li>
      <li><strong>Structured alert data</strong>, normalized fields: processes, commands, hashes, identifiers.</li>
      <li><strong>Validation results</strong>, the four-dimensional check on signal fidelity.</li>
      <li><strong>Telemetry correlation</strong>, cross-platform context tying the alert to its environment.</li>
    </ul>
  </div>

  <div class="deliverable-card">
    <h3>🧭 Leads for Subject and Uncover</h3>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>Baseline anomalies noticed</strong>, anything that looked off against casual knowledge of the entity. Subject owns the real behavioral analysis.</li>
      <li><strong>Correlations spotted in validation</strong>, related events worth chasing. Uncover owns the attack-chain reconstruction.</li>
      <li><strong>Intel hits captured</strong>, IOC or TTP matches recorded as leads, not conclusions. Uncover owns the fusion and attribution.</li>
      <li><strong>Temporal anchors</strong>, the timestamps that fix the chronology later phases build on.</li>
    </ul>
  </div>

  <div class="deliverable-card">
    <h3>🚩 Flags for Scope, Risk, and Escalation</h3>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>Possibly critical assets named</strong>, systems that look important. Scope classifies criticality; Alert just raises the flag.</li>
      <li><strong>Regulated-data proximity noted</strong>, signs the case may touch PCI, PHI, or PII scope. Scope's regulatory pillar does the mapping.</li>
      <li><strong>Early severity signals</strong>, observations Risk will weigh. No impact score is assigned here.</li>
      <li><strong>Escalation criteria in sight</strong>, if any pre-defined criterion already looks close to confirmed, say so now: a criterion confirmed at <em>any</em> phase escalates immediately under the <a href="/docs/escalation/criteria/">break-glass rule</a>.</li>
    </ul>
  </div>
</div>

## Preparing for Subject analysis

Alert told you *what fired*. Subject asks *who acted*. To make that question answerable, organize three things before you start.

### Entity identification

From the parsed Alert data, list every <DefineTerm term="Entity" display="entity" /> that touched the activity:

- **Primary entities**: users, systems, services directly named in the detection.
- **Secondary entities**: related accounts, connected systems, dependent services that may have been affected.
- **High-value targets**: critical assets or privileged accounts that could become targets for <DefineTerm term="Lateral Movement" display="lateral movement" />.

### Context requirements

The Subject phase needs organizational context the Alert phase didn't have to assemble:

- **<DefineTerm term="User" /> role information**: job functions, department, business responsibilities.
- **System classifications**: criticality ratings, data sensitivity, operational importance.
- **Relationship mappings**: network connectivity, access dependencies, trust relationships.

### Investigation parameters

Set the shape of the Subject-phase work before you start:

- **Analysis depth**: how much behavioral, authorization, authentication, and relationship analysis is the alert worth?
- **Time boundaries**: historical window for baseline comparison and pattern detection.
- **Escalation thresholds**: what would the Subject phase need to find to confirm an escalation criterion? Name it now, because confirmation at any phase fires the [break-glass rule](/docs/escalation/criteria/) immediately, without waiting for Risk.

<Callout variant="info" title="The handoff is the artifact">
If a peer picked up this alert tomorrow, would the Alert-phase work give them a clean starting point? That's the test. The transition isn't ceremony, it's quality assurance for everything downstream.
</Callout>

<KeyTakeaway>
A clean Alert phase ends with a structured, validated alert + an entity list + a context plan. The Subject phase doesn't re-derive that work. It builds on it.
</KeyTakeaway>
