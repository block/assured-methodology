## What ASSURED is, and is not

ASSURED is a triage methodology. Seven phases, Alert, Subject, Scope, Uncover, Risk, Escalation, Documentation, that take an analyst from "an alert fired" to "the case is closed or handed off, and the record survives." It is not an incident-response framework. It is not a SOAR playbook. It is the work an analyst does before either of those starts.

<Reveal summary="The methodology's four commitments" variant="info">

**Decisions are based on named inputs, not feelings.** Two analysts working the same alert produce comparable outputs because the inputs are explicit: which detection mechanism fired, which dimensions of Subject came back clean or dirty, which entities are in or out of scope, which ATT&CK techniques the chain maps to, which RATM dimensions score high.

**Roles and handoffs are explicit.** The triage analyst owns the verdict and the handoff packet. The incident responder owns the lifecycle. The SOC manager owns the closure review. When the handoff is structured, no role does the other's work.

**False-positives are first-class cases.** A documented close at triage carries the same evidentiary weight as a documented escalation. The methodology refuses the false-positive shrug: a full-arc investigation gets the nine-section event report whichever way the verdict lands, and even a Level 0 pattern close gets its five-field record (the <a href="/docs/fast-path/">fast path</a>). Depth follows the case; the discipline of writing it down does not.

**The loop is real.** Scope → Uncover → Risk iterates as new evidence surfaces. Refining a scope mid-case is the methodology working, not the methodology failing.

</Reveal>

<Callout variant="info" title="What ASSURED is, plainly">
Structured but not rigid. Thorough but not exhaustive. The methodology gives an analyst a place to stand on every alert that fires, and a record the team can read months later.
</Callout>

## Where to go from here

<div class="next-grid">
  <div class="next-card">
    <h3>🎯 Apply it to your next alert</h3>
    <p>The methodology is not a textbook; it is a practice. Use the structure on your next investigation and notice where you would otherwise have skipped a step. The patterns reveal themselves through use.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="next-card">
    <h3>🧑‍🏫 Teach it</h3>
    <p>The methodology was designed to be taught. Mentoring a newer analyst through their first ASSURED-structured case is one of the fastest ways to refine your own use of it; the <a href="/docs/teaching/">teaching guide</a> and <a href="/docs/exercises/">exercise packets</a> carry the materials. The act of explaining sharpens application.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="next-card">
    <h3>📈 Bring your team</h3>
    <p>Taking a whole SOC onto the methodology is a different project from adopting it yourself: it has a rollout sequence, a set of quality metrics, and a short list of duties only the team lead can carry. The <a href="/docs/adoption/">adoption guide</a> maps all three.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="next-card">
    <h3>🤝 Contribute</h3>
    <p>The methodology lives at <a href="https://github.com/block/assured-methodology" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">github.com/block/assured-methodology</a>. Feedback, corrections, and contributions are welcome: releases follow the repo's <a href="https://github.com/block/assured-methodology/blob/main/GOVERNANCE.md" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">versioning policy</a> and are recorded in the <a href="https://github.com/block/assured-methodology/blob/main/CHANGELOG.md" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">changelog</a>. The next version of ASSURED is shaped by the people who use it.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="next-card">
    <h3>📚 Keep the glossaries close</h3>
    <p>The three glossaries are built as ongoing references, not one-time reads: <a href="/docs/glossaries/bad/">B.A.D.</a> for adversary behaviors and tradecraft, <a href="/docs/glossaries/cat/">C.A.T.</a> for the tools and platforms of security operations, and <a href="/docs/glossaries/clear/">C.L.E.A.R.</a> for the shared vocabulary of triage itself.</p>
  </div>
</div>
