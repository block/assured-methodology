## What you have, across all seven chapters

Across seven chapters, the methodology gave you four things. A **vocabulary**: seven letters, each answering one question, a shared language that travels across teams and cases and survives turnover. A **structure**: three stages that group the seven phases, Orient (Alert, Subject, Scope), Investigate (Uncover, Risk), and Resolve (Escalation decision, Documentation), so the structure does the orienting and attention goes to the evidence. A **discipline**: each phase hands one deliverable forward, and a skipped phase shows up downstream, however small the case. And a **scaffold**: a map for new analysts and a checklist for experienced ones, which improves as cases reveal where it bends.

Each phase answers one question and hands one deliverable forward, and each deliverable lands somewhere specific in the record:

| Phase | Anchor question | Deliverable | Where it lands in the record |
|---|---|---|---|
| Alert | What fired, and why? | The validated alert and its detection context | 01 Event overview |
| Subject | Who is involved? | Entity assessments | 03 Origin and scope |
| Scope | Where are the lines? | The boundary: entities, time window, systems in and out | 03 Origin and scope |
| Uncover | What does the evidence say? | The evidence chain, with confidence labels and coverage gaps | 02 Timeline, 03, 06 Indicators and observables |
| Risk | How bad is it, and how confident are we? | Impact, likelihood, confidence, matrix cell, priority, and a recommended move | Decision header, 05 Business impact |
| Escalation decision | Close or escalate, and who needs to know? | The documented close or the handoff packet, plus the communication record | Decision header, 04 Actions taken, 09 Appendices |
| Documentation | What did we decide, and what did we learn? | The event report, reviewed and linked | 07 Lessons learned, 08 Post-event recommendations |

## What ASSURED is, and is not

ASSURED is a triage methodology: seven phases (Alert, Subject, Scope, Uncover, Risk, Escalation decision, Documentation) that take an analyst from "an alert fired" to "the case is closed or handed off, and the record survives." It is neither an incident-response framework nor a SOAR playbook but the analyst's decision work between those two points, building on whatever automation has already enriched and handing IR a structured starting point; the <a href="/docs/intro/">introduction</a> sets out the whole arc, the <a href="/docs/positioning/">positioning page</a> shows how it compares with the frameworks around it, and the <a href="/docs/standards-mapping/">standards mapping</a> shows where its records meet NIST CSF 2.0, ISO/IEC 27001 and the other frameworks an auditor tests against.

<Reveal summary="The methodology's four commitments" variant="info">

**Decisions are based on named inputs, not feelings.** Two analysts working the same alert should reach comparable outputs, because every input is explicit, from the detection logic that fired to the matrix cell, with confidence recorded beside the rating.

**Roles and handoffs are explicit.** Risk recommends; the Escalation decision decides close or escalate, and records it. The triage analyst owns that decision, the verdict, and the handoff packet. The incident responder owns the incident lifecycle once the packet is accepted. The SOC manager owns the closure review: per case for Level 2, P1/P2 and escalated-then-closed records, and through the weekly QA sample for Level 0 and 1 closes. When the handoff is structured, no role does the other's work.

**Benign closes are first-class cases.** A documented close carries the same evidentiary weight as a documented escalation. The methodology refuses the "just a false positive" shrug: a full-arc investigation gets the nine-section event report whichever way the verdict lands, and even a Level 0 pattern close gets its five-field record (the <a href="/docs/fast-path/">fast path</a>). Depth follows the case; the discipline of writing it down does not. Those records are also the audit evidence for CSF 2.0 DE.AE-08 and ISO/IEC 27001 A.5.25; the <a href="/docs/standards-mapping/">standards mapping</a> lists which record serves which outcome.

**The loop is real.** Scope → Uncover → Risk iterates as new evidence surfaces. Refining a scope mid-case is the methodology working, not the methodology failing.

</Reveal>
