# Five common failure modes

<div class="pit-grid">
  <div class="pit"><h4>📭 Incomplete records</h4><p>Fields left blank, sections skipped, references not linked. The record exists but does not actually support a future review. Often caused by writing after the case is closed rather than during.</p></div>
  <div class="pit"><h4>🕰️ Post-hoc rationalization</h4><p>Documentation written days later that records the verdict cleanly but omits the dead ends and alternative theories the analyst considered. The result reads tidy and is less useful for training.</p></div>
  <div class="pit"><h4>🔗 Missing evidence chains</h4><p>The record states a conclusion but does not say which evidence supports it. The next reader cannot verify the reasoning without redoing the work.</p></div>
  <div class="pit"><h4>🤐 Sanitization mistakes</h4><p>Records that contain PII, regulated data, or sensitive operational detail that should not be in the system the documentation lives in. The record is complete and unusable for compliance reasons.</p></div>
  <div class="pit"><h4>📜 Form over substance</h4><p>Long documentation that meets the standard but says nothing useful. The fields are filled; the value is absent. The template was treated as the goal.</p></div>
</div>

<Callout variant="info" title="The shortest fix">
The single biggest fix is writing during the investigation rather than after. Five minutes of "this is what I just observed" notes at each step produces a better record than two hours of reconstruction at the end. The methodology asks the analyst to treat documentation as a continuous part of the work.
</Callout>

# Driving value beyond the closure

Good documentation has uses beyond the single case:

- **Trend analysis.** Multiple records of the same alert type reveal campaigns, infrastructure, and shifts in adversary behavior.
- **Training.** New analysts learn faster from real, well-documented cases than from synthetic exercises.
- **Audit readiness.** A regulator's request is a query against the record set, not a fire drill.
- **Detection engineering.** Closed false positives feed tuning. Confirmed cases feed new detection rules.
- **Team retention.** Analysts who see their work documented and used stay longer than those whose work disappears the moment a case closes.

<KeyTakeaway>The pitfalls are predictable. Catching them is mostly a matter of writing during the investigation, keeping evidence chains tight, and treating the form as scaffolding rather than the goal.</KeyTakeaway>
