# Two templates worth standardizing

<div class="tpl-grid">
  <div class="tpl">
    <h4>📋 Alert report template</h4>
    <p>One template per closed alert. Fields: alert source, detection family, ATT&CK techniques observed, primary entities, validation summary, verdict, response actions, detection feedback. Filled during triage, finalized at closure.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="tpl">
    <h4>📅 Timeline tracker</h4>
    <p>Sequential record of events with timestamps. Two columns: adversary actions and analyst actions. The two columns side by side reveal cause and response together, which is what most post-mortems want to see.</p>
  </div>
</div>

# Why standardized templates matter

- **Speed.** Filling a template is faster than writing from a blank page. The discipline pays off across hundreds of cases.
- **Consistency.** Templates make two analysts' records look comparable, which is what trend analysis depends on.
- **Completeness.** Templates surface fields the analyst would otherwise forget. The structure does the remembering.
- **Tooling integration.** Templates can be encoded into the case-management system, the SOAR playbook, or the SIEM. Manual templates work; automated templates work better.

<KeyTakeaway>A template is not a constraint; it is scaffolding. Templates make documentation faster, more consistent, and more complete. Every minute spent designing the right template saves hours of inconsistent records.</KeyTakeaway>
