## Why standardized templates

A template is the difference between "what should I write?" and "fill in the next field" during an active case. The structure carries the completeness burden: every field the record needs is prompted, including the ones analysts forget under pressure, like negative findings and decision rationale. Consistency is what makes records comparable across cases; three phishing reports with the same fields can reveal a campaign; three free-form narratives cannot.

In practice that means building the templates into the case-management system rather than a separate document, validating the critical fields so a case cannot close with them blank, and keeping one specialized variant per common event type instead of one giant form.

<Callout variant="info" title="Customize the core template by event type">
The core template ships here and stays universal across the organization; specialized variants are built on top of it, and should exist for the common categories: <DefineTerm term="Phishing" display="phishing" />, <DefineTerm term="Credential" display="credential" /> compromise, <DefineTerm term="Ransomware" display="ransomware" />, insider misuse, <DefineTerm term="Data Exfiltration" display="data exfiltration" />. Two variants ship with the core. The <a href="/templates/alert-report-phishing.md" download>phishing variant</a> adds the message and its email <DefineTerm term="Artifacts" display="artifacts" /> (sender and authentication results), the recipients and delivery path through the mail <DefineTerm term="Infrastructure" display="infrastructure" />, the URL and attachment verdicts, and the click evidence. The <a href="/templates/alert-report-identity.md" download>identity-compromise variant</a> adds the session ID, the sign-in chain, the MFA ceremony, consent grants, mailbox rules, and the revocation order. Both paste into the core alert report after the activity summary; the core's fields do not change. Ransomware templates capture encryption details, ransom demands, and <DefineTerm term="Business Continuity" display="business continuity" /> impacts.
</Callout>

## The alert reporting template

A well-designed alert report template captures all essential details consistently to support rapid understanding, <DefineTerm term="Event Correlation" display="event correlation" />, and knowledge reuse. Each report includes the following core fields.

Eight fields is a deliberate ceiling for the universal core, not a starting point; the specialized variants above add their event-type fields on top, but the core itself does not grow. Every field added to the core raises the cost of every report the team will ever write, and the fields skipped under pressure are exactly the discriminating ones. Of the eight, two do most of the retrospective work: the initial triage outcome, because it records what the analyst believed with the evidence available at the time, and the escalation decision, because it records the judgment call a reviewer is most likely to second-guess. Fill those two with care; the rest is largely mechanical capture.

<div class="alert-tpl">
  <div class="alert-tpl__row">
    <div class="alert-tpl__field">Event title and identifier</div>
    <div class="alert-tpl__desc">Concise yet descriptive. Pairs with a unique identifier (e.g., CASE-YYYYMMDD-NNN; an INC- identifier is added only if the case is declared an incident) for chronological tracking and cross-referencing.</div>
  </div>
  <div class="alert-tpl__row">
    <div class="alert-tpl__field">Detection timestamp and source</div>
    <div class="alert-tpl__desc">Standardized UTC timestamp with optional local time. Detection source specifies the tool or control (EDR, SIEM, CSPM) responsible for generating the alert.</div>
  </div>
  <div class="alert-tpl__row">
    <div class="alert-tpl__field">Activity summary</div>
    <div class="alert-tpl__desc">Describes the observable behavior that triggered the alert. Balances technical clarity with readability, highlighting specific indicators and anomalies.</div>
  </div>
  <div class="alert-tpl__row">
    <div class="alert-tpl__field">Initial triage outcome</div>
    <div class="alert-tpl__desc">Early classification on two axes: detection (true positive, false positive (logic), or false positive (data)) and activity (malicious, policy violation, authorized (benign true positive), benign-confirmed, or undetermined), with concise justification grounded in observable behaviors and detection logic.</div>
  </div>
  <div class="alert-tpl__row">
    <div class="alert-tpl__field">Affected systems</div>
    <div class="alert-tpl__desc">Endpoints, accounts, infrastructure elements involved, with business context (function, criticality, ownership).</div>
  </div>
  <div class="alert-tpl__row">
    <div class="alert-tpl__field">Risk assessment</div>
    <div class="alert-tpl__desc">What Risk produced: impact, likelihood that the activity is malicious or unauthorized, confidence in that judgment, the matrix cell and priority (P1-P4), and a recommended move. Risk recommends; the escalation decision below decides.</div>
  </div>
  <div class="alert-tpl__row">
    <div class="alert-tpl__field">Actions taken</div>
    <div class="alert-tpl__desc">Investigative or containment steps with timestamps and outcomes. Telemetry reviews, process analysis, network validation, remediation.</div>
  </div>
  <div class="alert-tpl__row">
    <div class="alert-tpl__field">Escalation decision and final disposition</div>
    <div class="alert-tpl__desc">Whether the case was escalated, to whom, and why, or why it was closed, with who decided and when (UTC). Final disposition on both axes, plus status: closed, escalated, or merged into a case.</div>
  </div>
</div>

## Timeline and action tracking

Maintaining an accurate, timestamped timeline is fundamental to effective documentation. The timeline provides a sequential record of activities, decisions, and communications, supporting retrospective analysis and accountability.

Each timeline entry should contain five components. The five exist because a timeline that only answers "what happened when" is half a record. Timestamp, actor, and action reconstruct the sequence, and anyone with the raw logs could rebuild those. Rationale and outcome are the two components the logs cannot supply, and they are the reason the timeline is written by the analyst instead of exported from the SIEM: rationale preserves why the step made sense at the time, and outcome records whether reality cooperated.

<div class="timeline-grid">
  <div class="timeline-card">
    <span class="timeline-card__num">01</span>
    <h3>Timestamp</h3>
    <p>The exact time the action occurred, in UTC with the date (RFC 3339, e.g. 2026-03-03T09:11:41Z), for consistency across geographies and shifts.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="timeline-card">
    <span class="timeline-card__num">02</span>
    <h3>Actor</h3>
    <p>Who performed the action: a named human (with role), an automated system (SOAR or platform automation, EDR, IAM), or an AI agent (product and version, plus the human who authorized the action).</p>
  </div>
  <div class="timeline-card">
    <span class="timeline-card__num">03</span>
    <h3>Action taken</h3>
    <p>Brief but descriptive: "isolated host via EDR," "blocked IP in firewall," "added hash to denylist." Concrete and specific.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="timeline-card">
    <span class="timeline-card__num">04</span>
    <h3>Rationale</h3>
    <p>Reasoning behind the action, especially for discretionary steps. Documents the decision-making process and justification.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="timeline-card">
    <span class="timeline-card__num">05</span>
    <h3>Outcome / result</h3>
    <p>Observed effect: successful containment, error encountered, lack of expected response. Captures actual vs. intended.</p>
  </div>
</div>

<Callout variant="info" title="Real-time, collaborative timelines">
Timelines should be maintained in real time and synchronized across the systems involved in the event lifecycle. Case-management and SOAR platforms (e.g. TheHive, DFIR-IRIS, Microsoft Sentinel incidents, Google SecOps cases) and forensic timeline tools (e.g. Timesketch) support shared, attributed timeline entries, so several responders can add to one <DefineTerm term="Incident Response" display="incident response" /> timeline. These integrated timelines become the authoritative record of event progression, supporting handoffs, <a href="/docs/operating-variants/#shift-handover">shift changes</a>, and distributed-team coordination.
</Callout>

## Copy-ready templates

Everything above describes the templates; these are the templates themselves. Pick by depth and outcome:

| Case | What the Escalation decision writes | What Documentation finalizes |
|---|---|---|
| Level 0 pattern close | The five-field pattern-close record, at close (the close is an early Escalation decision) | Nothing further: the five-field record is the whole record |
| Level 1 standard triage | The alert report's escalation and disposition fields | The alert report, written as you go and finalized at close |
| Level 1 phishing or identity alert | The alert report's escalation and disposition fields | The alert report with the phishing or identity-compromise variant pasted in after the activity summary |
| Level 2, closed | The closure record: the decision header, the closure check with its findings, the named gaps and reopen triggers, and the communication trail | The nine-section event report, assembled from the case record under that header |
| Any Level 2 case, while it is worked | Nothing yet: the case is still open | The case record, opened at Alert and filled phase by phase: the Subject handoff, the Scope boundary statement and the Risk verdict block land in it at each transition, and the packet or report is assembled from it |
| Any escalation | The handoff packet, at escalation | The triage-side event report, mapping the packet in and linking IR's outputs |
| Any case still open at a shift change | Nothing yet: the case is still open | The shift handover, at every shift change (one per customer for an MSSP or MDR), linking each open case record rather than replacing it |
| A Level 2, P1/P2 or escalated-then-closed record before it is submitted | Nothing further | The closure review, run by a reviewer who is not the record's author (Level 0 and 1 closes go through the weekly QA sample instead) |

Whichever artifact applies, the investigative entries lock at handoff or close; corrections and reopenings are appended with author, time, and reason, never overwritten (see <a href="/docs/documentation/standards/#amending">amending a locked record</a>). The Cursor alert report above is the alert-level artifact; its facts later open that case's event report. Each block below is rendered from the shipped file, so what you copy is exactly what you download. Each is plain Markdown: paste it into a case management system, a ticket, or a shared doc, or download the file and drop it into the team's template library. Every field name and enum value in them is defined in the <a href="/docs/glossaries/assured/">ASSURED terms glossary</a>, and the verdict fields use its <a href="/docs/glossaries/assured/#verdicts">verdict vocabulary</a> exactly. Lines marked optional (depth level, grouped member alerts, distribution, regulatory flags, the hypothesis ledger) are what make a record shareable and countable later; leave them out only when they do not apply. The optional regulatory flags block in the handoff packet and the event report records, for each regime that may apply, the data class and trigger observed, the candidate awareness timestamp, and when the owner was engaged; triage records the facts a clock may start from, and the regime's owner decides whether it started (see the <a href="/docs/scope/clocks/#flags-block">flags block on the notification-clocks page</a>). The alert report's timeline uses the five components above (timestamp, actor, action taken, rationale, outcome). The handoff packet's and the event report's timelines add a Source and a Confidence column, so each row names the record it came from and its label on the shared rubric, and their hypothesis ledgers carry a provenance column (the source and query behind the deciding evidence).

<p><strong>Which template when:</strong> the case record opens at Alert and gains one section per phase; the handoff packet and the event report are assembled from it, not written from scratch. Inside it, the phase records land in order: the <a href="/docs/subject/transition/">Subject handoff</a> at the end of Subject, the <a href="/docs/scope/transition/">Scope boundary statement</a> at the Scope transition, pinned above Uncover's query window, and the <a href="/docs/risk/impact-likelihood/#verdict-block">Risk verdict block</a> at the end of Risk. The closure review is the reviewer's checklist for a Level 2, P1/P2 or escalated-then-closed record before it is submitted; Level 0 and 1 closes go through the weekly QA sample instead. The shift handover goes out at every shift change, one per customer for an MSSP or MDR.</p>

The escalation-criteria checklist is the run-at-every-phase companion to these: the four canonical categories as checkable items, with the escalation-tripwire decision rule at the bottom. The tuning request is what Documentation files to detection engineering after a verdict; the <a href="/docs/detection-feedback/#the-routing-table">routing table</a> picks its request type and owner for each verdict. The monthly triage report rolls the records' key times and verdicts up into the team-level numbers described on <a href="/docs/measuring-triage/">Measuring triage</a>. ### Machine-readable schemas

Every record template also ships as a JSON Schema (draft 2020-12) at `/templates/schema/<name>.schema.json`, linked from each template block below. The verdict, case-status, priority, matrix-cell, confidence and depth-level enums are defined once, in the shared <a href="/templates/schema/assured-vocabulary.schema.json">vocabulary schema</a>, and every template schema points at them, so a SOAR playbook or a case tool can load the fields and pick lists directly instead of retyping them. The build fails if a template, its schema and the vocabulary drift apart, so the schema always describes what the Markdown asks for.

- **OCSF:** the schemas describe case records, not events. Keep the raw events in your <DefineTerm term="OCSF" /> store (or ECS, ASIM or UDM) and cite them from the record by event ID. Where the platform models findings in OCSF, the record's detection-axis and activity-axis verdicts and its priority map onto the Detection Finding and Incident Finding classes; check the attribute names against the OCSF version you ingest.
- **STIX:** the indicators-and-observables section exports as <DefineTerm term="Structured Threat Information Expression (STIX)" display="STIX" /> 2.1 Indicator or Observed Data objects, with the record's TLP marking carried as a STIX marking definition. ATT&CK technique IDs keep the ATT&CK version they were mapped against.
- **Versioning:** the published schemas describe the current edition's templates, and every template download carries a "Template version" line naming that edition. Renaming or removing a field or an enum value changes the meaning of an existing deliverable, so under the repository's <a href="https://github.com/block/assured-methodology/blob/main/GOVERNANCE.md" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">versioning policy</a> it ships only in a major version, and the <a href="https://github.com/block/assured-methodology/blob/main/CHANGELOG.md" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">changelog</a> lists every field change.
