# E: Escalation - Triage to Broader Response

The Escalation phase is the sixth component of the ASSURED methodology, focusing on determining when escalation is necessary and establishing appropriate escalation paths for comprehensive incident response. This phase bridges the gap between initial event triage and broader incident response, ensuring that security events receive appropriate attention and resources based on their risk assessment.

## Overview

The Escalation phase provides structured escalation management through:
- Clear escalation criteria and thresholds
- Internal and external escalation protocols
- Incident command structure establishment
- Communication and coordination frameworks
- Resource allocation and management

## Criteria for Escalation

Establishing clear escalation criteria ensures that security events are escalated appropriately and consistently across the organization.

### Automatic Escalation Triggers

**Risk-Based Triggers**
- Critical risk events (Score: 9-10)
- High risk events with business impact
- Regulatory notification requirements
- Executive or VIP involvement
- Media attention or public disclosure

**Technical Triggers**
- Active attack in progress
- Data exfiltration confirmed
- Lateral movement detected
- Privilege escalation achieved
- Persistence mechanisms established

**Business Impact Triggers**
- Critical system compromise
- Customer data exposure
- Financial loss exceeding thresholds
- Operational disruption
- Reputational risk

**Compliance Triggers**
- Regulatory notification deadlines
- Audit and investigation requirements
- Legal and litigation implications
- Industry-specific obligations
- Contract and SLA impacts

### Escalation Decision Framework

**Risk Assessment Integration**
- Risk score thresholds
- Impact severity levels
- Likelihood assessments
- Business context considerations
- Resource availability factors

**Stakeholder Impact Analysis**
- Executive and board involvement
- Customer and partner notification
- Regulatory and legal requirements
- Media and public relations
- Industry and peer communication

**Resource Requirements**
- Technical expertise needs
- External vendor support
- Management oversight requirements
- Legal and compliance involvement
- Communication and coordination needs

## Event Triage vs Incident Response

Understanding the distinction between event triage and incident response is crucial for appropriate escalation and resource allocation.

### Event Triage Characteristics

**Scope and Duration**
- Limited scope and timeframe
- Single or isolated events
- Standard response procedures
- Routine monitoring and documentation
- Local team handling

**Resource Requirements**
- Standard analyst resources
- Routine tools and systems
- Normal business hours coverage
- Standard documentation
- Basic communication

**Decision Authority**
- Team lead decision-making
- Standard procedure following
- Routine escalation protocols
- Local stakeholder communication
- Standard reporting

### Incident Response Characteristics

**Scope and Duration**
- Broad scope and extended timeframe
- Multiple related events
- Customized response procedures
- Enhanced monitoring and documentation
- Cross-functional team involvement

**Resource Requirements**
- Specialized expertise and resources
- Advanced tools and systems
- 24/7 coverage and support
- Comprehensive documentation
- Enhanced communication

**Decision Authority**
- Executive decision-making
- Strategic procedure development
- Enhanced escalation protocols
- External stakeholder communication
- Comprehensive reporting

## Working Example: Escalation

### Scenario: PowerShell Execution Investigation

**Risk Assessment Results:**
- Risk Score: 8 (High Risk)
- Business Impact: High
- Data Exposure: Confirmed
- Threat Actor: Known APT group

**Escalation Decision:**
- Automatic escalation to Level 2 (Security Management)
- External vendor engagement required
- Legal and compliance involvement needed
- Executive notification required

**Escalation Process:**

**Level 1 to Level 2:**
- Security team lead notified management
- Risk assessment shared with management
- Resource requirements identified
- External vendor engagement initiated

**Level 2 to Level 3:**
- Executive briefing prepared
- Business impact assessment completed
- Legal and compliance coordination initiated
- External communication planning started

**External Escalation:**
- Incident response vendor engaged
- Legal counsel consulted
- Compliance team notified
- Customer notification prepared

**Command Structure Activation:**
- Incident Commander: Security Director
- Technical Lead: Senior Security Analyst
- Communications Lead: Communications Manager
- Legal Lead: General Counsel

## Transition to Documentation Phase

After completing the Escalation phase, the investigation transitions to the **Documentation** phase, where analysts will:

- Document all escalation decisions and rationale
- Record communication and coordination activities
- Maintain comprehensive incident records
- Ensure compliance and audit requirements
- Preserve knowledge for future reference

The escalation decisions and coordination established in this phase provide the framework for comprehensive documentation in the final phase.

## Key Takeaways

1. **Clear Criteria**: Well-defined escalation criteria ensure consistent and appropriate escalation
2. **Structured Protocols**: Internal and external escalation protocols provide clear guidance
3. **Command Structure**: Incident command structure ensures effective coordination
4. **Communication**: Clear communication protocols enable effective stakeholder management
5. **Documentation**: Thorough documentation of escalation decisions ensures accountability

The Escalation phase provides the critical transition from event triage to comprehensive incident response, ensuring that security events receive appropriate attention and resources based on their risk assessment and business impact.
