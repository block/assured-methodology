# Five canonical escalation triggers

<div class="trig-grid">
  <div class="trig"><h4>✅ Confirmed malicious activity</h4><p>Evidence rules out alternative explanations. The case is no longer "possibly an incident"; it is one.</p></div>
  <div class="trig"><h4>🏢 Business-critical systems affected</h4><p>Production, customer-facing, revenue-generating, or compliance-bound systems are in scope. Even if the evidence is incomplete, the impact trigger applies.</p></div>
  <div class="trig"><h4>↔️ Lateral movement detected</h4><p>The adversary moved beyond the initial entry point. Containment becomes urgent because the blast radius is growing.</p></div>
  <div class="trig"><h4>📤 Data exfiltration detected</h4><p>Confirmed or strongly suspected outbound transfer of sensitive data. Regulatory notification timelines often start here.</p></div>
  <div class="trig"><h4>🌐 Ongoing or widespread campaign</h4><p>Multiple hosts, multiple users, multiple sectors. The case is no longer a single intrusion to investigate.</p></div>
</div>

<Callout variant="info" title="High impact OR high likelihood, not just both">
A pre-defined criterion is met when either dimension crosses the threshold. A critical-asset alert with medium likelihood still escalates because the cost of being wrong is too high to absorb on a Tier 1 desk.
</Callout>

<KeyTakeaway>Escalation criteria are the SOC's pre-committed answer to "should I escalate?" Use them as the default, override only with a documented reason.</KeyTakeaway>
