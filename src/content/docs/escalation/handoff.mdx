## What the packet contains

<div class="packet-list">
  <div class="pl"><h4>📌 1. Case summary</h4><p>One paragraph. What happened, what is known, what the triage analyst recommends.</p></div>
  <div class="pl"><h4>⏰ 2. Timeline</h4><p>Sequential events with timestamps. Includes both adversary actions and analyst actions (when notified, what was checked, when escalated).</p></div>
  <div class="pl"><h4>👥 3. Entities</h4><p>Primary and secondary entities with their assessment results. The Subject and Scope output.</p></div>
  <div class="pl"><h4>🔍 4. Evidence chain</h4><p>The Uncover narrative with sources, queries used, and <DefineTerm term="MITRE ATT&CK" display="MITRE" /> technique mappings.</p></div>
  <div class="pl"><h4>⚖️ 5. Risk verdict</h4><p>Impact, likelihood, combined priority, open questions, and recommended response.</p></div>
  <div class="pl"><h4>📦 6. Containment actions taken</h4><p>What <DefineTerm term="Containment" display="containment" /> was already done (host isolated, account disabled, etc.) and what was deliberately not done and why.</p></div>
  <div class="pl"><h4>📎 7. Artifacts</h4><p>Relevant log excerpts, <DefineTerm term="Indicators of Compromise (IoC)" display="IoCs" />, hashes, screenshots, queries. Attached or linked so the next analyst does not have to re-derive them. Each artifact carries its custody and preservation state: where the original lives, its identifying hash, and who has handled it, per the <a href="/docs/documentation/standards/">chain-of-custody procedure</a>. If the case ends up in front of a regulator or a court, this field is what keeps the evidence usable.</p></div>
  <div class="pl"><h4>🚧 8. Open questions</h4><p>What the triage analyst could not resolve and would have pursued next. The IR team picks up from this list.</p></div>
  <div class="pl"><h4>📞 9. Communication record</h4><p>Who has been notified so far, on which channel, when. Manager paged at 14:02. Compliance flagged at 14:18. Vendor request opened at 14:35. Prevents duplicate notifications, gives IR an instant view of who already knows, and starts the audit trail for regulatory reporting.</p></div>
</div>

<Callout variant="info" title="The packet is documentation">
The handoff packet is also the documentation record. Months later, this packet is what an auditor or a post-incident reviewer will read. Writing it for clarity (not just brevity) pays off the moment someone needs to understand the case without the analyst present.
</Callout>

## What makes a packet actionable

A complete packet still fails if it does not pass a few practical tests. The methodology asks the analyst to verify three properties before declaring a packet ready for handoff.

<div class="actionable-tests">
  <div class="actionable-test">
    <h4>📖 Can be read in five minutes</h4>
    <p>The case summary plus the Risk verdict should give the receiving tier the picture in under five minutes. Detail lives in the appendix. The opening should orient, not exhaust.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="actionable-test">
    <h4>🎯 Names the next decision</h4>
    <p>A good packet does not just describe the case; it tees up the decision the next tier needs to make. "Isolate the second host," "engage compliance," "contain the federated session": concrete recommendations.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="actionable-test">
    <h4>🪞 Survives the cold read</h4>
    <p>An analyst who was not involved should be able to open the packet, understand the case, and continue the work. If the packet requires the triage analyst's presence to interpret, it is not actionable yet.</p>
  </div>
</div>

<Reveal summary="A packet that fails the cold-read test" variant="warn">

A common anti-pattern: the packet reads as a sequence of analyst thoughts ("I checked X, looked OK, then I checked Y, found something weird"). The narrative makes sense to the person who wrote it. Someone reading it cold has no idea what was concluded.

The fix is structural. Replace the analyst-narrative voice with the nine-section format above. Each section answers a specific question; the reader can navigate them without reading the others. The case summary at the top tells the reader whether they need to read the rest at all.

Test the packet by handing it to a peer who has no context on the case. If they cannot summarize the verdict in one sentence after five minutes, the packet is not done.

</Reveal>

<KeyTakeaway>The handoff packet is the triage analyst's deliverable. Nine named sections, each one a documented answer to a question the next tier would otherwise have to ask. Three practical tests: readable in five minutes, names the next decision, survives the cold read.</KeyTakeaway>
