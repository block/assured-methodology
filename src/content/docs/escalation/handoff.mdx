# What the packet contains

<div class="packet-list">
  <div class="pl"><h4>📌 Case summary</h4><p>One paragraph. What happened, what is known, what the triage analyst recommends.</p></div>
  <div class="pl"><h4>⏰ Timeline</h4><p>Sequential events with timestamps. Includes both adversary actions and analyst actions (when notified, what was checked, when escalated).</p></div>
  <div class="pl"><h4>👥 Entities</h4><p>Primary and secondary entities with their assessment results. The Subject and Scope output.</p></div>
  <div class="pl"><h4>🔍 Evidence chain</h4><p>The Uncover narrative with sources, queries used, and MITRE technique mappings.</p></div>
  <div class="pl"><h4>⚖️ Risk verdict</h4><p>Impact, likelihood, combined priority, open questions, and recommended response.</p></div>
  <div class="pl"><h4>📦 Containment actions taken</h4><p>What was already done (host isolated, account disabled, etc.) and what was deliberately not done and why.</p></div>
  <div class="pl"><h4>📎 Artifacts</h4><p>Relevant log excerpts, IoCs, hashes, screenshots, queries. Attached or linked so the next analyst does not have to re-derive them.</p></div>
  <div class="pl"><h4>🚧 Open questions</h4><p>What the triage analyst could not resolve and would have pursued next. The IR team picks up from this list.</p></div>
</div>

<Callout variant="info" title="The packet is documentation">
The handoff packet is also the documentation record. Months later, this packet is what an auditor or a post-incident reviewer will read. Writing it for clarity (not just brevity) pays off the moment someone needs to understand the case without the analyst present.
</Callout>

<KeyTakeaway>The handoff packet is the triage analyst's deliverable. Eight named sections, each one a documented answer to a question the next tier would otherwise have to ask.</KeyTakeaway>
