<Callout variant="info" title="What the Escalation decision is for">
Risk scores the case and recommends a move; the Escalation decision makes the call and records it. A documented close is a full outcome, held to the same evidence standard as an escalation. When the answer is escalate, Escalation is not just "send it to the IR team." It is the deliberate, documented handoff of the work the triage analyst has done so far, in a form the next tier can pick up without re-investigating. Done well, escalation makes <DefineTerm term="Incident Response" display="incident response" /> start from where triage finished. Done poorly, it costs the IR team hours of rework; teams that <a href="/docs/measuring-triage/">measure their triage</a> track exactly that rework as a first-class signal.
</Callout>

## The Escalation decision at a glance

<PhaseAtAGlance phase="escalation">
  <Fragment slot="takes-in">Risk's priority band, matrix cell, confidence, and recommended move, plus any criterion flagged in an earlier phase.</Fragment>
  <Fragment slot="deliverable">A closure record for a documented close, or a <a href="/docs/escalation/handoff/">handoff packet</a> for an escalation. Either way, the decision with its time, its route, and its reasoning, and a communication record of who was told what.</Fragment>
  <Fragment slot="done">The decision is recorded in the E fields; a close has passed the <a href="/docs/escalation/criteria/#closing">closure check</a>, or an escalation has been accepted by a named receiver; every notification is in the communication record.</Fragment>
  <Fragment slot="level-0">The pattern close is an early Escalation decision, recorded as field 5 of the <a href="/docs/fast-path/#five-field">five-field record</a>. No packet and no notifications beyond the record.</Fragment>
  <Fragment slot="level-1">The E rule applied to Risk's recommendation in a few minutes: a documented close that passes the closure check, or an escalation with a packet.</Fragment>
  <Fragment slot="level-2">The full chapter: the E rule, the closure check or the packet with a warm handoff, the stakeholder map, and preservation before any containment.</Fragment>
  <Fragment slot="any-level">A confirmed criterion is an early Escalation decision from whatever phase the case is in: the <a href="/docs/escalation/criteria/#tripwire">escalation tripwire</a>.</Fragment>
</PhaseAtAGlance>

Four reasons it is a phase of its own, rather than the last line of Risk:

- **The record is the artifact.** An escalation is worth what the next tier can act on without re-investigating; a close is worth what a future reader can trust about the absence of compromise.
- **Triage and IR are different work.** Confusing them produces premature escalation (IR doing triage's job) or delayed escalation (triage trying to do IR's). This is where the analyst decides which work the case now needs.
- **Criteria beat judgment under pressure.** Pre-defined criteria are the SOC's collective judgment encoded ahead of time, so a pressured analyst does not hesitate on a case that should have escalated an hour ago.
- **Paths outside the SOC exist.** IT operations, legal, HR, communications, an insurer, a vendor, a customer, a regulator through counsel, or law enforcement through counsel: this phase knows which one applies and when.

<KeyTakeaway>The Escalation decision closes the case or hands it on, and records why either way. A documented close is a first-class verdict; an escalation turns the triage analyst's work into someone else's starting point. The cleaner the record, the better the next decision.</KeyTakeaway>
