<Callout variant="info" title="What Escalation is for">
Escalation is not just "send it to the IR team." It is the deliberate, documented handoff of the work the triage analyst has done so far, in a form the next tier can pick up without re-investigating. Done well, escalation makes incident response start from where triage finished. Done poorly, it costs the IR team hours of rework.
</Callout>

# Why Escalation is its own phase

<div class="why-subject-grid">
  <FlipCard icon="📦" title="The handoff is the artifact" accent="indigo">
    The triage analyst's most consequential output is not "we found something." It is the structured packet that lets the next tier act without re-investigating. The packet is the artifact, not the alert.
  </FlipCard>
  <FlipCard icon="🧭" title="Triage and IR are different work" accent="indigo">
    Triage frames the puzzle. IR completes it. Confusing the two leads to either premature escalation (IR doing triage's job) or delayed escalation (triage trying to do IR's). Escalation is the phase where the analyst decides which work this is.
  </FlipCard>
  <FlipCard icon="🚪" title="Criteria beat judgment under pressure" accent="indigo">
    Pre-defined escalation criteria prevent the case where a stressed analyst hesitates on something that should have escalated five minutes ago. Criteria are the SOC's collective judgment encoded ahead of time.
  </FlipCard>
  <FlipCard icon="📋" title="External paths exist" accent="indigo">
    Sometimes the escalation goes to a vendor, to legal, to law enforcement, to a parent company's SOC, or to the customer. Escalation is the phase that knows which path applies and when.
  </FlipCard>
</div>

<KeyTakeaway>Escalation is the phase where the triage analyst's work becomes someone else's starting point. The cleaner the handoff, the better the response.</KeyTakeaway>
