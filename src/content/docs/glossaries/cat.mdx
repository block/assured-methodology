  The vendor and platform vocabulary an analyst meets in the security operations center (SOC). Each tool is defined by what it does: threat-intel feed, endpoint detection and response (EDR), security information and event management (SIEM), security orchestration, automation and response (SOAR), identity and access management (IAM), forensics, network security. The name on a vendor's slide maps back to a category an analyst can reason about, and each card says what the tool gives you in triage and where it can mislead you.

  <p class="cat-map__note"><strong>Reading a card:</strong> the first paragraph says what the tool is (it is also the hover definition used across the site). <strong>In triage</strong> names the evidence or action it gives you; <strong>Watch for</strong> names one blind spot. Headings carry the current product name. Parentheses in a heading only ever hold a short form, a former name ("formerly"), a new home ("now in"), or mark a category card ("concept"); vendor and ownership live in the small line at the foot of each card, with the source when a date is claimed. When a product was renamed, acquired, deprecated or retired, a status line under it gives the change and the date it was last checked. For what each family can and cannot see in an investigation, see <a href="/docs/field-reference/tool-families/">Tool families</a>.</p>

<section id="intel" class="cat-section">
  <header class="cat-section__head">
    <span class="cat-section__ico" aria-hidden="true"><svg viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2" stroke-linecap="round" stroke-linejoin="round" focusable="false"><path d="M4 11a9 9 0 0 1 9 9"/><path d="M4 4a16 16 0 0 1 16 16"/><circle cx="5" cy="19" r="1"/></svg></span>
    <div>
      <h2>Advisories, feeds &amp; sharing communities</h2>
      <p>Sources of curated indicators, vulnerability status and sector warnings. A feed tells you what someone else saw; it does not tell you what happened in your environment.</p>
      <p class="cat-section__phases">Vocabulary for: <a href="/docs/uncover/threat-intelligence/">Uncover</a> · <a href="/docs/risk/impact-likelihood/">Risk</a></p>
    </div>
  </header>
  <div class="cat-tools">
    <article id="abuse-ch" class="cat-tool"><h3>Abuse.ch</h3><p>Malware-infrastructure tracking project (a research project at Bern University of Applied Sciences; Spamhaus is its primary data licensee). Platforms: MalwareBazaar (samples), URLhaus (malware URLs), ThreatFox (indicators of compromise, IOCs), Feodo Tracker (botnet command-and-control, C2), SSLBL (malicious certificates and JA3) and YARAify, plus a unified Hunting search. Free for commercial and non-commercial use; the APIs take a free personal Auth-Key.</p><p class="cat-tool__use"><strong>In triage:</strong> a fast check of a hash, URL or C2 address against community malware tracking.</p><p class="cat-tool__use"><strong>Watch for:</strong> the old Ransomware Tracker is long gone; absence from these lists is not evidence of safety.</p><p class="cat-tool__meta">Operator: abuse.ch (Bern University of Applied Sciences)</p><VendorStatus id="abuse-ch" /><a class="g-cite" href="https://abuse.ch/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Source: abuse.ch</a></article>
    <article id="cisa-advisories" class="cat-tool"><h3>CISA Advisories</h3><p>U.S. Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency advisories on vulnerabilities and active threats, with mitigation guidance.</p><p class="cat-tool__use"><strong>In triage:</strong> joint advisories list indicators and ATT&amp;CK-mapped behavior for active campaigns; match them to the case's observables.</p><p class="cat-tool__use"><strong>Watch for:</strong> advisory indicators age fast; behavior sections outlast the IP lists.</p><p class="cat-tool__meta">Operator: CISA (U.S. government) · Also: <a href="#cisa-kev">CISA KEV</a></p><VendorStatus id="cisa-advisories" /></article>
    <article id="cisa-kev" class="cat-tool"><h3>CISA KEV</h3><p>The Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog: CVEs CISA has evidence of being exploited in the wild, each with a remediation due date for U.S. federal agencies.</p><p class="cat-tool__use"><strong>In triage:</strong> an exploitation attempt against a KEV-listed CVE on an unpatched, reachable asset is the combination the Risk phase treats as most urgent.</p><p class="cat-tool__use"><strong>Watch for:</strong> KEV lists what is known to be exploited, not everything that is; a missing entry is not a clean bill.</p><p class="cat-tool__meta">Operator: CISA</p><VendorStatus id="cisa-kev" /><a class="g-cite" href="https://www.cisa.gov/known-exploited-vulnerabilities-catalog" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Source: CISA KEV catalog</a></article>
    <article id="e-isac" class="cat-tool"><h3>E-ISAC</h3><p>Electricity sector ISAC; threat intel, IR support, and risk analysis tailored to grid operators.</p><p class="cat-tool__use"><strong>In triage:</strong> sector-specific campaign context for OT and utility environments.</p><p class="cat-tool__use"><strong>Watch for:</strong> sharing is governed by TLP markings; check them before an indicator leaves the case.</p><p class="cat-tool__meta">Operator: NERC (North American Electric Reliability Corporation)</p><VendorStatus id="e-isac" /></article>
    <article id="fbi-flash-reports" class="cat-tool"><h3>FBI Flash Reports</h3><p>Rapid high-priority notifications about imminent cyber threats targeting public and private partners.</p><p class="cat-tool__use"><strong>In triage:</strong> technical indicators and TTPs for a specific, current threat.</p><p class="cat-tool__use"><strong>Watch for:</strong> distribution is often TLP:AMBER or narrower; respect the marking.</p><p class="cat-tool__meta">Operator: FBI (U.S. government)</p><VendorStatus id="fbi-flash-reports" /></article>
    <article id="first-epss" class="cat-tool"><h3>FIRST EPSS</h3><p>The Exploit Prediction Scoring System: a daily probability, per CVE, that the vulnerability will be exploited in the wild in the next 30 days.</p><p class="cat-tool__use"><strong>In triage:</strong> a second exploitability signal next to KEV when an attempt targets a CVE.</p><p class="cat-tool__use"><strong>Watch for:</strong> EPSS describes the population of attacks, not your asset; it says nothing about reachability or whether the attempt succeeded.</p><p class="cat-tool__meta">Operator: FIRST (Forum of Incident Response and Security Teams)</p><VendorStatus id="first-epss" /><a class="g-cite" href="https://www.first.org/epss/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Source: FIRST EPSS</a></article>
    <article id="fs-isac" class="cat-tool"><h3>FS-ISAC</h3><p>Global financial-sector ISAC providing real-time alerts, incident coordination, and member collaboration.</p><p class="cat-tool__use"><strong>In triage:</strong> peer sightings of fraud and intrusion campaigns aimed at financial institutions.</p><p class="cat-tool__use"><strong>Watch for:</strong> member-shared indicators vary in confidence; corroborate before blocking.</p><p class="cat-tool__meta">Operator: FS-ISAC (member organization)</p><VendorStatus id="fs-isac" /></article>
    <article id="h-isac" class="cat-tool"><h3>Health-ISAC</h3><p>Healthcare-sector ISAC; threat intelligence, incident response coordination, and best practices for clinical environments.</p><p class="cat-tool__use"><strong>In triage:</strong> campaign context for attacks on hospitals, medical devices and health data.</p><p class="cat-tool__use"><strong>Watch for:</strong> sector reports can lag the first wave; pair them with your own telemetry.</p><p class="cat-tool__meta">Operator: Health-ISAC (member organization)</p><VendorStatus id="h-isac" /></article>
    <article id="alienvault-otx" class="cat-tool"><h3>LevelBlue Open Threat Exchange (OTX, formerly AlienVault OTX)</h3><p>Collaborative threat-intel exchange aggregating IOCs from a global community, with SIEM/automated integrations. Free community "pulses" of IOCs; quality varies by contributor, so corroborate before acting.</p><p class="cat-tool__use"><strong>In triage:</strong> a quick look at whether an indicator appears in a community pulse, and alongside what.</p><p class="cat-tool__use"><strong>Watch for:</strong> pulses are rarely retired, so old infrastructure lingers as "malicious".</p><p class="cat-tool__meta">Vendor: LevelBlue · Formerly: AlienVault OTX (AT&amp;T Cybersecurity)</p><VendorStatus id="alienvault-otx" /><a class="g-cite" href="https://otx.alienvault.com/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Source: LevelBlue OTX</a></article>
    <article id="ms-isac" class="cat-tool"><h3>MS-ISAC</h3><p>Multi-State ISAC for U.S. state, local, tribal, and territorial governments; alerts, IR, and risk management resources. Operated by the Center for Internet Security (CIS). DHS/CISA funding ended after 30 September 2025, and MS-ISAC moved to fee-based membership, so check current membership terms.</p><p class="cat-tool__use"><strong>In triage:</strong> SLTT-specific advisories and, for members, incident support.</p><p class="cat-tool__use"><strong>Watch for:</strong> services an SLTT team relied on before October 2025 may now sit behind a paid tier.</p><p class="cat-tool__meta">Operator: Center for Internet Security (CIS)</p><VendorStatus id="ms-isac" /><a class="g-cite" href="https://www.cisecurity.org/ms-isac/ms-isac-membership-faq" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Source: CIS MS-ISAC FAQ</a></article>
    <article id="nvd-euvd" class="cat-tool"><h3>NVD / EUVD</h3><p>Public vulnerability databases: the U.S. National Vulnerability Database (NIST) and the EU Vulnerability Database (ENISA). Both enrich CVE records with severity scores, affected products and references.</p><p class="cat-tool__use"><strong>In triage:</strong> what a CVE in an alert actually affects, and whether the targeted product and version match.</p><p class="cat-tool__use"><strong>Watch for:</strong> NVD enrichment has run with a backlog, so a fresh CVE may carry no score yet; read the vendor advisory too.</p><p class="cat-tool__meta">Operators: NIST (NVD) · ENISA (EUVD)</p><VendorStatus id="nvd-euvd" /><a class="g-cite" href="https://euvd.enisa.europa.eu/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Source: ENISA EUVD</a></article>
    <article id="ncsc-uk" class="cat-tool"><h3>UK NCSC</h3><p>UK National Cyber Security Centre; guidance, threat intelligence, and IR services across public and private sectors.</p><p class="cat-tool__use"><strong>In triage:</strong> advisories and joint reports with indicators and behavior for active campaigns.</p><p class="cat-tool__use"><strong>Watch for:</strong> joint advisories are often co-published; one campaign, several URLs.</p><p class="cat-tool__meta">Operator: UK government (part of GCHQ)</p><VendorStatus id="ncsc-uk" /></article>

{/* 2. Intel platforms and research */}
<section id="tip" class="cat-section">
  <header class="cat-section__head">
    <span class="cat-section__ico" aria-hidden="true"><svg viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2" stroke-linecap="round" stroke-linejoin="round" focusable="false"><path d="M2 3h6a4 4 0 0 1 4 4v14a3 3 0 0 0-3-3H2z"/><path d="M22 3h-6a4 4 0 0 0-4 4v14a3 3 0 0 1 3-3h7z"/></svg></span>
    <div>
      <h2>Threat-intel platforms, research &amp; knowledge bases</h2>
      <p>A threat-intelligence platform (TIP) ingests many feeds, deduplicates and scores their indicators, and matches them against your own telemetry. Research teams and knowledge bases supply the context behind the indicators.</p>
      <p class="cat-section__phases">Vocabulary for: <a href="/docs/uncover/threat-intelligence/">Uncover</a></p>
    </div>
  </header>
  <div class="cat-tools">
    <article id="anomali-threatstream" class="cat-tool"><h3>Anomali ThreatStream</h3><p>Commercial TIP: collects, analyzes, and prioritizes threat data with enrichment and risk scoring; integrates with existing security stacks.</p><p class="cat-tool__use"><strong>In triage:</strong> one place to see every feed that lists an indicator, with a merged confidence score.</p><p class="cat-tool__use"><strong>Watch for:</strong> a merged score inherits the weakest feed's errors; open the sources.</p><p class="cat-tool__meta">Vendor: Anomali</p><VendorStatus id="anomali-threatstream" /></article>

      <p>The free and paid services an analyst queries one indicator at a time: reputation, registration, scan history, rendered pages, decoding.   </header>

    prompt="A domain admin account logged on to a server at 02:00. Which tool tells you whether that use of the credential was checked out and recorded?"
    options={[
      { value: "iga",   label: "An identity governance (IGA) platform that certifies who holds which access." },
      { value: "pam",   label: "A privileged access management (PAM) vault with checkout and session logs." },
      { value: "sso",   label: "The SSO identity provider, which logs each federated sign-in to SaaS apps." },

    answer="pam"
    explanation="A PAM vault such as CyberArk or BeyondTrust records who checked out a privileged credential and, with session recording, what they did. A privileged logon with no matching checkout is the finding. IGA records who should have access, not each use. An SSO provider sees federated SaaS sign-ins, not a domain logon to a server. Endpoint management shows software deployment, not credential use."

    prompt="Your team subscribes to six threat feeds and keeps getting duplicate, unscored indicators. Which category is built to fix that?"

      { value: "isac",    label: "An ISAC, which shares sector advisories and indicators among its members." },

    answer="tip"
    explanation="A TIP such as MISP, OpenCTI or Anomali ThreatStream ingests many feeds, deduplicates and scores indicators, and pushes the useful ones to detection tools. A SIEM matches indicators against your events but is not built to curate intelligence. An ISAC is one more source of feed data. A sandbox produces indicators from a sample but does not manage them."
