<div class="intro-lede">
  <div class="intro-lede__primary">
    A security alert is a question the detection engine is asking the analyst. <em>Is this real, is it malicious, how serious is it, and what should happen next?</em>
  </div>

  <div class="intro-lede__secondary">
    Event triage is the work of answering those questions with confidence, and it is a distinct skill from broader incident response. ASSURED is a structured, repeatable methodology for doing that work consistently. The seven letters of <strong>A</strong>lert, <strong>S</strong>ubject, <strong>S</strong>cope, <strong>U</strong>ncover, <strong>R</strong>isk, <strong>E</strong>scalation, and <strong>D</strong>ocumentation cover the full arc of a triage investigation from the moment an alert fires to the moment the analyst hands off (or closes) the case.
  </div>
</div>

<Callout variant="info" title="Why ASSURED, why now">
<DefineTerm term="Incident Response" display="Incident response" /> frameworks like NIST 800-61, SANS PICERL, and <DefineTerm term="MITRE ATT&CK" /> describe what to do once you already know what you are looking at. <DefineTerm term="Event Triage" display="Event triage" /> is the work before that, and most analysts learn it informally on the job. ASSURED gives that informal craft a structure that scales: scaffolding for newer analysts, a checklist for experienced ones, and a shared vocabulary for handoff and review.
</Callout>

<Callout variant="warn" title="Triage is not incident response">
ASSURED is a triage methodology. The triage analyst answers four questions — *is this real, is it malicious, how serious is it, what should happen next* — and either closes the case or hands it to incident response. The IR responder runs the formal lifecycle (NIST 800-61: preparation → detection &amp; analysis → containment → eradication → recovery → post-incident). Triage produces the **decision** to escalate; IR runs the **response** once the decision is made. The two roles share telemetry and vocabulary; they are not the same job. The clearer the line between them, the cleaner the handoff. See the **<a href="/docs/escalation/triage-vs-ir">Triage vs. IR</a>** page in the Escalation chapter for the full distinction.
</Callout>

<Callout variant="info" title="The loop is intentional">
Triage is not strictly linear. Risk findings often send the analyst back to Uncover for more evidence. Uncover sometimes reveals new subjects the original alert never mentioned. Discovering a new subject can change the scope of the entire investigation. The dashed feedback arrow in the diagram represents that flow, and embracing it is part of the methodology.
</Callout>

<div class="intro-section-head">
  <span class="section-overline">Why event triage deserves its own method</span>
  <h2 class="intro-section-title">The six recurring problems ASSURED is shaped by</h2>
  <p class="intro-section-lede">
    Security analysts at every level of experience meet these problems regularly. Each phase of ASSURED is designed to address one or more of them directly.
  </p>
</div>

<div class="intro-feature-grid">
  <article class="intro-feature">
    <div class="intro-feature__icon" aria-hidden="true">📊</div>
    <h3>Data overload</h3>
    <p>SOCs see thousands of alerts per day from heterogeneous sources, and a small fraction describe real threats. Without a way to separate signal from noise, the events that matter get buried in volume. <strong>Addressed by:</strong> Alert and Validation.</p>
  </article>

  <article class="intro-feature">
    <div class="intro-feature__icon" aria-hidden="true">⚙️</div>
    <h3>Tooling sprawl</h3>
    <p>Telemetry lives across multi-cloud environments, hybrid datacenters, container platforms, and serverless functions. Cross-tool correlation does not happen by default. <strong>Addressed by:</strong> Uncover.</p>
  </article>

  <article class="intro-feature">
    <div class="intro-feature__icon" aria-hidden="true">🎯</div>
    <h3>Adversaries adapt</h3>
    <p>Modern intrusions use living-off-the-land techniques, polymorphic payloads, and social engineering. The activity looks like normal operations. <strong>Addressed by:</strong> behavioral analysis throughout the methodology, especially in Subject and Validation.</p>
  </article>

  <article class="intro-feature">
    <div class="intro-feature__icon" aria-hidden="true">📋</div>
    <h3>Regulatory pressure</h3>
    <p>Investigations now carry compliance obligations alongside the security work. GDPR, PCI DSS, HIPAA, SOX each impose requirements on documentation, notification, and handling. <strong>Addressed by:</strong> Scope and Documentation.</p>
  </article>

  <article class="intro-feature">
    <div class="intro-feature__icon" aria-hidden="true">🧠</div>
    <h3>Cognitive load</h3>
    <p>Analysts juggle SIEM, SOAR, EDR, identity systems, and cloud consoles within a single investigation. Context switching erodes accuracy and accelerates fatigue. <strong>Addressed by:</strong> the methodology's structure itself, which gives the analyst a clear next step at every moment.</p>
  </article>

  <article class="intro-feature">
    <div class="intro-feature__icon" aria-hidden="true">📝</div>
    <h3>Documentation gaps</h3>
    <p>Inconsistent records make post-mortems painful, trend analysis impossible, and audits stressful. <strong>Addressed by:</strong> Documentation, which is the final phase precisely because capturing decisions is what makes the work durable.</p>
  </article>
</div>

<div class="intro-section-head">
  <span class="section-overline">Goals of the methodology</span>
  <h2 class="intro-section-title">What ASSURED is designed to deliver</h2>
  <p class="intro-section-lede">
    Four outcomes the methodology aims for at every step. Together they describe what a SOC running ASSURED looks like when it is working well.
  </p>
</div>

<div class="intro-goal-grid">
  <article class="intro-goal">
    <div class="intro-goal__num">01</div>
    <div class="intro-goal__icon" aria-hidden="true">🤖</div>
    <h3>Strategic automation</h3>
    <p>Each phase highlights what is safe to automate and what requires human judgment. Automation handles the repetitive work (enrichment, correlation, scoring) so analysts can spend their time on the decisions that actually need a person.</p>
  </article>

  <article class="intro-goal">
    <div class="intro-goal__num">02</div>
    <div class="intro-goal__icon" aria-hidden="true">📐</div>
    <h3>Process consistency</h3>
    <p>Two analysts working the same alert with the same methodology produce comparable outputs. That makes training easier, escalation cleaner, and audits less stressful. The shared vocabulary is the consistency layer.</p>
  </article>

  <article class="intro-goal">
    <div class="intro-goal__num">03</div>
    <div class="intro-goal__icon" aria-hidden="true">🧭</div>
    <h3>Reduced cognitive load</h3>
    <p>The methodology gives the analyst a structured set of questions to work through at each step, so they are not asking "what do I do next" even on unfamiliar alert types. The structure does the orienting; attention goes to the evidence.</p>
  </article>

  <article class="intro-goal">
    <div class="intro-goal__num">04</div>
    <div class="intro-goal__icon" aria-hidden="true">📈</div>
    <h3>Scales with the team</h3>
    <p>ASSURED works for a one-person SOC and for a follow-the-sun team of forty. Feedback loops keep the framework usable as detection logic evolves and the team grows. The method does not depend on team size or tooling specifics.</p>
  </article>
</div>

<div class="intro-section-head">
  <span class="section-overline">Time budget at a glance</span>
  <h2 class="intro-section-title">On-shift target per phase (not reading time)</h2>
  <p class="intro-section-lede">
    These are <strong>operational targets</strong> for a live shift — how long each phase should take when you're triaging an actual alert. They are <strong>not</strong> reading-time estimates; absorbing the chapter material the first time takes substantially longer (a careful first read of the full site runs ~7–10 hours including quizzes and examples). A well-run mid-complexity triage finishes in roughly 55–135 minutes end-to-end. Complex cases legitimately take longer; trivial false-positive closes can be far shorter. The point is having a felt sense of when to slow down, when to escalate early, and when "I've been here too long" is the signal.
  </p>
</div>

<Callout variant="info" title="When you blow past the budget">
Running long on a single phase is a signal, not a failure. Twenty minutes into Alert and still unclear what the detection logic saw? That is usually a sign the alert came from a system you do not yet understand — escalate to a peer or grab a senior analyst. Ninety minutes into Uncover with no convergence? Stop, write down what you know, and either ask for help or formally escalate as "still investigating." The budget exists so you notice these moments.
</Callout>
