A security alert is a question the detection engine is asking. The analyst's job is to answer it: is this real, is it malicious, how serious is it, and what should happen next? Event triage is the work of answering those questions with confidence, and it is a distinct skill from broader incident response. ASSURED is a structured, repeatable methodology for doing that work consistently.

The methodology gets its name from the seven components an analyst moves through: **A**lert, **S**ubject, **S**cope, **U**ncover, **R**isk, **E**scalation, and **D**ocumentation. Each one answers a single, specific question. Together they cover the full arc of a triage investigation, from the moment an alert fires to the moment the analyst hands off (or closes) the case.

<Callout variant="info" title="Why ASSURED, why now">
Incident response frameworks like NIST 800-61, SANS PICERL, and MITRE ATT&CK describe what to do once you already know what you are looking at. Event triage is the work before that, and most analysts learn it informally on the job. ASSURED gives that informal craft a structure that scales: scaffolding for newer analysts, a checklist for experienced ones, and a shared vocabulary for handoff and review.
</Callout>

<Callout variant="info" title="The loop is intentional">
Triage is not strictly linear. Risk findings often send the analyst back to Uncover for more evidence. Uncover sometimes reveals new subjects the original alert never mentioned. Discovering a new subject can change the scope of the entire investigation. The dashed feedback arrow in the diagram represents that flow, and embracing it is part of the methodology.
</Callout>

# Why event triage deserves its own method

Security analysts at every level of experience face the same recurring problems. ASSURED is shaped by those realities, and each phase is designed to address one or more of them directly. Tap any card below to read the full picture.

<div class="intro-flips">
  <FlipCard icon="📊" title="Data overload" accent="blue">
    Modern SOCs see thousands of alerts per day from heterogeneous sources, and a small fraction of them describe real threats. Without a structured way to separate signal from noise, the events that matter get buried in the volume. ASSURED's Alert and Validation steps focus on reducing that noise quickly.
  </FlipCard>

  <FlipCard icon="⚙️" title="Tooling sprawl" accent="teal">
    Telemetry lives across multi-cloud environments, hybrid datacenters, container platforms, and serverless functions. The data correlates poorly across tool boundaries by default. ASSURED's Uncover phase makes that cross-tool correlation a deliberate step, not an afterthought.
  </FlipCard>

  <FlipCard icon="🎯" title="Adversaries adapt" accent="red">
    Modern intrusions use living-off-the-land techniques, polymorphic payloads, and social engineering. The activity looks like normal operations. ASSURED's behavioral and contextual analysis is designed to catch what static signatures cannot.
  </FlipCard>

  <FlipCard icon="📋" title="Regulatory pressure" accent="purple">
    Investigations now carry compliance obligations alongside the security work. GDPR, PCI-DSS, HIPAA, and SOX each impose requirements on documentation, notification, and handling. The Scope and Documentation phases make those obligations explicit at the right step.
  </FlipCard>

  <FlipCard icon="🧠" title="Cognitive load" accent="orange">
    Analysts juggle SIEM, SOAR, EDR, identity systems, and cloud consoles, sometimes within a single investigation. Context switching erodes accuracy and accelerates fatigue. ASSURED is designed to reduce mental overhead by giving each step a single question to answer.
  </FlipCard>

  <FlipCard icon="📝" title="Documentation gaps" accent="indigo">
    Inconsistent records make post-mortems painful, trend analysis impossible, and audits stressful. The Documentation phase is the final step for a reason: capturing decisions and reasoning is what makes the work survive its closure.
  </FlipCard>
</div>

# Goals of the methodology

<div class="intro-flips">
  <FlipCard icon="🤖" title="Strategic automation" accent="teal">
    Each phase highlights what is safe to automate and what requires human judgment. Automation handles the repetitive work (enrichment, correlation, scoring) so analysts can spend their time on the decisions that actually need a person.
  </FlipCard>

  <FlipCard icon="📐" title="Process consistency" accent="blue">
    Two analysts working the same alert with the same methodology produce comparable outputs. That makes training easier, escalation cleaner, and audits less stressful. The shared vocabulary is the consistency layer.
  </FlipCard>

  <FlipCard icon="🧭" title="Reduced cognitive load" accent="green">
    Each component has a clear question to answer. Analysts are not asking "what do I do next" because the methodology tells them, even on an unfamiliar alert type. The structure does the orienting so the analyst can spend their attention on the evidence.
  </FlipCard>

  <FlipCard icon="📈" title="Scales with the team" accent="indigo">
    ASSURED works for a one-person SOC and for a follow-the-sun team of forty. Feedback loops keep the framework usable as detection logic evolves and as the team grows. The method does not depend on team size or tooling specifics.
  </FlipCard>
</div>
