## Four complementary frameworks

<div class="fw-grid">
  <a href="#ratm" class="fw-card"><h4>🎚️ RATM <span class="fw-card__role">output</span></h4><p>Risk-Based Alert Triage Matrix. Composite rating across asset criticality, threat intent, exploitation scope, and business impact. The verdict you write in the case.</p></a>
  <a href="#pace" class="fw-card"><h4>🧭 P.A.C.E. <span class="fw-card__role">process</span></h4><p>Potential impact, Actor sophistication, Context, Escalation criteria. The four-step think-through that gets you to the verdict.</p></a>
  <a href="#mitre" class="fw-card"><h4>🎯 MITRE ATT&CK <span class="fw-card__role">vocabulary</span></h4><p>Maps observed behaviors to a shared taxonomy of adversary tactics, techniques, and procedures.</p></a>
  <a href="#cvss" class="fw-card"><h4>📈 CVSS <span class="fw-card__role">severity</span></h4><p>Standardized numerical severity for vulnerabilities, adapted for active-threat prioritization.</p></a>
</div>

<Callout variant="info" title="Why standardize the assessment">
Frameworks make Risk repeatable across analysts and incidents. They reduce variability from individual judgment, create a shared vocabulary for handoff, and accelerate onboarding for new analysts. Organizations adapt these frameworks to industry, <DefineTerm term="Regulatory Requirements" display="regulatory requirements" />, and risk tolerance, but the structural benefits hold across customizations.
</Callout>

<Callout variant="info" title="P.A.C.E. vs. RATM, process and output">
The two look similar at a glance. They are not redundant. **P.A.C.E. is the *process***, a four-step think-through (Potential impact → Actor sophistication → Context → Escalation criteria) that walks an analyst from raw observation to a defensible verdict. **RATM is the *output***, a composite rating produced by scoring the case across four dimensions (asset criticality, threat intent, exploitation/scope, business impact). Use P.A.C.E. to *arrive* at a position; use RATM to *document and communicate* it. A mature SOC runs P.A.C.E. in the analyst's head and records the result as a RATM score in the case notes.
</Callout>

<h2 id="ratm">🎚️ Risk-Based Alert Triage Matrix</h2>

The RATM scores alerts across four dimensions, producing a composite rating. It emphasizes technical reality while integrating business context.

<div class="ratm-grid">
  <div class="ratm-card">
    <h4>Asset criticality</h4>
    <p>Is the affected system, user, or service essential to operations, or does it hold sensitive data? High-value assets (production environments, financial systems, PII repositories) warrant greater concern. Requires understanding of both technical function and business value. Inventories with criticality ratings must be reviewed and updated, or risk assessments will misrepresent actual business impact.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="ratm-card">
    <h4>Threat actor intent</h4>
    <p>Is the alert opportunistic behavior or targeted activity? Sophisticated, deliberate techniques raise priority. Indicators of intent include reconnaissance focused on specific high-value targets, custom malware designed to evade detection, or attack patterns suggesting familiarity with the organization's architecture.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="ratm-card">
    <h4>Exploitation and scope</h4>
    <p>Did the exploit succeed? Is lateral movement, persistence, or privilege escalation possible? Verified compromise or multi-host activity escalates risk significantly. The scope dimension evaluates both the current footprint and the potential to expand. Evidence of successful exploitation (command execution, data access, configuration changes) raises the rating.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="ratm-card">
    <h4>Business impact</h4>
    <p>What downstream effects on availability, integrity, or confidentiality? Could legal or regulatory consequences follow? Business disruption or reputational harm elevates response priority. Translates technical events into terms organizational leadership recognizes: continuity, customer trust, contractual obligations, financial exposure.</p>
  </div>
</div>

### Scoring rubric: how to convert observations into High / Medium / Low

The four dimensions above are the categories. The table below is the operational rubric, what counts as **High**, **Medium**, or **Low** for each. Calibrate the thresholds to your environment, but start from these defaults so two analysts working the same case land at the same score.

<div class="ratm-rubric">
  <div class="ratm-rubric__row ratm-rubric__row--header">
    <span>Dimension</span><span>High</span><span>Medium</span><span>Low</span>
  </div>
  <div class="ratm-rubric__row">
    <span class="ratm-rubric__dim">Asset criticality</span>
    <span>Crown-jewel system; regulated-data store (PCI, PHI, PII); revenue-affecting service.</span>
    <span>Business-critical but not regulated; internal-tooling backbone; broad-access shared service.</span>
    <span>Dev / sandbox / test environment; user-class endpoint with no privileged role; isolated workstation.</span>
  </div>
  <div class="ratm-rubric__row">
    <span class="ratm-rubric__dim">Threat actor intent</span>
    <span>Confirmed targeted activity; TTPs map to a named threat group (APT/ransomware); custom tooling; insider attribution.</span>
    <span>Commodity tooling with some targeting (spear-phish, region/role-specific lure); off-the-shelf C2 framework; financially-motivated opportunist.</span>
    <span>Opportunistic / automated; mass scan; commodity malware on a single endpoint; no targeting signal.</span>
  </div>
  <div class="ratm-rubric__row">
    <span class="ratm-rubric__dim">Exploitation &amp; scope</span>
    <span>Confirmed lateral movement; persistence established; multiple systems compromised; privilege escalation succeeded.</span>
    <span>Single system compromised, no spread confirmed yet; exploitation succeeded but agent contained; credential theft without observed reuse.</span>
    <span>Initial access only / attempted; no execution observed; signature match without behavioral confirmation; exposure without exploitation.</span>
  </div>
  <div class="ratm-rubric__row">
    <span class="ratm-rubric__dim">Business impact</span>
    <span>Regulatory notification clock triggered (GDPR 72h, HIPAA, PCI); revenue-affecting outage; customer-data exposure confirmed.</span>
    <span>Operational disruption possible (team-level); internal-data exposure; brand / customer-trust risk if disclosed.</span>
    <span>Contained, no business effect observed; sandbox-only; visible only to security team.</span>
  </div>
</div>

<Callout variant="info" title="Calibration note">
The thresholds above are starting defaults. Two adjustments most teams make: (1) move *Asset criticality* High to match your actual regulated-asset inventory, not a textbook one, if you have no PCI footprint, "regulated-data store" needs a different anchor; (2) treat *Threat actor intent* High as a *confidence-gated* upgrade, if attribution is speculative, score Medium and note the upgrade path in the RATM record.
</Callout>

<h2 id="pace">🧭 P.A.C.E. Model</h2>

P.A.C.E. is a step-by-step model that guides analysts through four key dimensions. It helps evaluate consequences, <DefineTerm term="Threat Actor" display="threat actor" /> sophistication, context, and predefined escalation criteria to determine whether immediate action or further investigation is required.

<div class="pace-grid">
  <div class="pace-card">
    <span class="pace-card__letter">P</span>
    <h4>Potential impact</h4>
    <p>Evaluates the potential consequences if the event remains unresolved. Data breach, system downtime, reputational harm, each raises prioritization.</p>
    <ul class="pace-card__bullets">
      <li>Measures severity of possible outcomes</li>
      <li>Considers both short and long-term effects</li>
      <li>Maps to business continuity concerns</li>
    </ul>
  </div>
  <div class="pace-card">
    <span class="pace-card__letter">A</span>
    <h4>Actor sophistication</h4>
    <p>Assesses skill level and resources. Amateur group or APT? Higher sophistication signals a more dangerous threat.</p>
    <ul class="pace-card__bullets">
      <li>Evaluates technical complexity of observed TTPs</li>
      <li>Considers attribution to known threat groups</li>
      <li>Examines customization versus off-the-shelf tooling</li>
    </ul>
  </div>
  <div class="pace-card">
    <span class="pace-card__letter">C</span>
    <h4>Context</h4>
    <p>Examines evidence supporting alert legitimacy. Match to known attack patterns or correlation with related IOCs raises priority.</p>
    <ul class="pace-card__bullets">
      <li>Correlates activity with historical patterns</li>
      <li>Considers normal vs. abnormal for users / systems</li>
      <li>Incorporates threat intel and current campaign awareness</li>
    </ul>
  </div>
  <div class="pace-card">
    <span class="pace-card__letter">E</span>
    <h4>Escalation criteria</h4>
    <p>Defines clear triggers for when an alert should escalate. Predefined thresholds, significant operational impact, evidence aligned with known threat models.</p>
    <ul class="pace-card__bullets">
      <li>Applies organization-specific thresholds</li>
      <li>Ensures consistent decision-making</li>
      <li>Supports defensible triage outcomes</li>
    </ul>
  </div>
</div>

<h2 id="mitre">🎯 MITRE ATT&CK as a risk lens</h2>

ATT&CK enters Risk as a structuring vocabulary, not a scoring tool. The framework's value at this phase is naming what was observed so the verdict aligns with the broader <DefineTerm term="Threat" display="threat" /> landscape.

<div class="mitre-risk-grid">
  <div class="mitre-risk-card">
    <h4>Tactic-level severity weighting</h4>
    <p>Some tactics carry inherently higher risk than others. Initial Access without follow-on activity is lower risk than confirmed Lateral Movement or Exfiltration. The tactic chain itself shapes priority.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="mitre-risk-card">
    <h4>Technique-to-actor mapping</h4>
    <p>Procedures associated with specific threat actors raise the risk weight. Custom tooling or rare techniques generally indicate targeted activity rather than commodity attacks.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="mitre-risk-card">
    <h4>Coverage gap indicators</h4>
    <p>When the observed chain includes techniques the SOC does not yet have detection for, that gap itself is a risk signal, both for this case and for the program.</p>
  </div>
</div>

<Callout variant="info" title="ATT&CK alone is not the verdict">
Mapping to <DefineTerm term="MITRE ATT&CK" /> is necessary but not sufficient. The framework names what happened; it does not say how much it matters in this environment for this asset. Pair the ATT&CK mapping with the RATM or P.A.C.E. lens to translate technique-level observations into business-level priority.
</Callout>

<h2 id="cvss">📈 CVSS adaptation for triage</h2>

<DefineTerm term="CVSS">Common Vulnerability Scoring System. A numerical score (0 to 10) describing the severity of a vulnerability across exploitability, impact, and contextual factors. Originally designed for vulnerability management; adapted for active-threat prioritization.</DefineTerm> applies structured methodology to assess risk. By considering attack vector, complexity, and the system's impact on confidentiality, integrity, and availability, CVSS provides a standardized approach to prioritize alerts.

The numerical scoring system (0–10) provides clear thresholds for escalation, with scores grouped into qualitative severity ratings (Low, Medium, High, Critical).

<div class="cvss-grid">
  <div class="cvss-card cvss-card--base">
    <span class="cvss-card__tag">BASE</span>
    <h4>Base score</h4>
    <p>Reflects the intrinsic severity. Attack vector, privileges required, impact on confidentiality / integrity / availability. A higher score typically indicates a more critical alert.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="cvss-card cvss-card--temporal">
    <span class="cvss-card__tag">TEMPORAL</span>
    <h4>Temporal score</h4>
    <p>Modifies the base score based on current exploitation trends and available mitigation techniques. Adjusts urgency as the threat landscape evolves.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="cvss-card cvss-card--env">
    <span class="cvss-card__tag">ENVIRONMENTAL</span>
    <h4>Environmental score</h4>
    <p>Tailors the score to the organization's infrastructure, business priorities, and existing controls. Evaluates whether critical systems are at risk and whether compensating controls reduce overall exposure.</p>
  </div>
</div>

<Callout variant="warn" title="When CVSS misleads">
A CVSS 9.8 on an asset that is not exposed, not in use, or already mitigated by compensating controls deserves a different response than the same score on a production system. Use CVSS as input, then weight it by the context Subject and Scope provided. The base score is a starting point; the environmental score is what matters for the triage decision.
</Callout>

## The triage matrix in practice

The four frameworks combine into a composite verdict. The triage matrix below is the practical output: impact and likelihood combine into a default action.

  <div class="matrix-row matrix-row--header">
    <span>Impact</span><span>Likelihood</span><span>Default action</span>
  </div>
  <div class="matrix-row"><span>High</span><span>High</span><span class="matrix-row__action matrix-row__action--p1">Escalate immediately</span></div>
  <div class="matrix-row"><span>High</span><span>Medium</span><span class="matrix-row__action matrix-row__action--p2">Escalate within shift</span></div>
  <div class="matrix-row"><span>High</span><span>Low</span><span class="matrix-row__action matrix-row__action--p3">Continue investigation; raise priority if evidence strengthens</span></div>
  <div class="matrix-row"><span>Medium</span><span>High</span><span class="matrix-row__action matrix-row__action--p2">Escalate within shift</span></div>
  <div class="matrix-row"><span>Medium</span><span>Medium</span><span class="matrix-row__action matrix-row__action--p3">Investigate to closure within 24 hours</span></div>
  <div class="matrix-row"><span>Medium</span><span>Low</span><span class="matrix-row__action matrix-row__action--p4">Standard queue</span></div>
  <div class="matrix-row"><span>Low</span><span>High</span><span class="matrix-row__action matrix-row__action--p3">Investigate; likely false-positive, but worth a clean close</span></div>
  <div class="matrix-row"><span>Low</span><span>Medium</span><span class="matrix-row__action matrix-row__action--p4">Standard queue; close after light investigation</span></div>
  <div class="matrix-row"><span>Low</span><span>Low</span><span class="matrix-row__action matrix-row__action--p5">Document and close</span></div>
</div>

<KeyTakeaway>
The Risk framework produces a verdict the analyst can defend. RATM provides the multi-dimensional scoring. P.A.C.E. structures the assessment. ATT&CK names the techniques. CVSS quantifies <DefineTerm term="Vulnerability" display="vulnerability" /> severity. The combination is what makes the verdict repeatable, defensible, and portable across analysts.
</KeyTakeaway>
