## One scoring model, two supporting lenses

<div class="fw-grid">
  <a href="#ratm" class="fw-card"><h4>🎚️ RATM <span class="fw-card__role">scoring</span></h4><p>Risk-Based Alert Triage Matrix, ASSURED's own scoring model. Four scoring dimensions with a High/Medium/Low rubric, reduced to the impact and likelihood axes of the triage matrix. The verdict you write in the case.</p></a>
  <a href="#mitre" class="fw-card"><h4>🎯 MITRE ATT&CK <span class="fw-card__role">vocabulary</span></h4><p>Maps observed behaviors to a shared taxonomy of adversary tactics, techniques, and procedures. Names what happened; does not score it.</p></a>
  <a href="#cvss" class="fw-card"><h4>📈 CVSS <span class="fw-card__role">severity</span></h4><p>Standardized numerical severity for vulnerabilities. An input to the RATM when the alert is exploit-driven, not a verdict on its own.</p></a>
</div>

<Callout variant="info" title="What is borrowed and what is ours">
A structured assessment makes Risk repeatable across analysts and incidents: it reduces variability from individual judgment, creates a shared vocabulary for handoff, and accelerates onboarding. Two of the lenses here are industry standards, <DefineTerm term="MITRE ATT&CK" /> and <DefineTerm term="Common Vulnerability Scoring System (CVSS)" display="CVSS" />. **The RATM is this methodology's own construct**, not an external standard; adapt its rubric to your industry, <DefineTerm term="Regulatory Requirements" display="regulatory requirements" />, and risk tolerance. What matters is that the whole team scores from the same calibrated rubric, whatever its anchors say.
</Callout>

<h2 id="ratm">Risk-Based Alert Triage Matrix</h2>

The RATM scores alerts across four scoring dimensions, producing a composite rating. It emphasizes technical reality while integrating business context. The four scoring dimensions reduce to the two axes of the triage matrix at the bottom of this page: **asset criticality + business impact make up the impact axis**; **threat actor intent + exploitation &amp; scope, weighted by evidence quality, make up the likelihood axis**. Score the dimensions, reduce them to the two axes, read the default action off the matrix.

<div class="ratm-grid">
  <div class="ratm-card">
    <h4>Asset criticality</h4>
    <p>Is the affected system, user, or service essential to operations, or does it hold sensitive data? High-value assets (production environments, financial systems, PII repositories) warrant greater concern. Requires understanding of both technical function and business value. Inventories with criticality ratings must be reviewed and updated, or risk assessments will misrepresent actual business impact.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="ratm-card">
    <h4>Threat actor intent</h4>
    <p>Is the alert opportunistic behavior or targeted activity? Sophisticated, deliberate techniques raise priority. Indicators of intent include reconnaissance focused on specific high-value targets, custom malware designed to evade detection, or attack patterns suggesting familiarity with the organization's architecture.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="ratm-card">
    <h4>Exploitation and scope</h4>
    <p>Did the exploit succeed? Is lateral movement, persistence, or privilege escalation possible? Verified compromise or multi-host activity escalates risk significantly. The scope dimension evaluates both the current footprint and the potential to expand. Evidence of successful exploitation (command execution, data access, configuration changes) raises the rating.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="ratm-card">
    <h4>Business impact</h4>
    <p>What downstream effects on availability, integrity, or confidentiality? Could legal or regulatory consequences follow? Business disruption or reputational harm elevates response priority. Translates technical events into terms organizational leadership recognizes: continuity, customer trust, contractual obligations, financial exposure.</p>
  </div>
</div>

### Scoring rubric: how to convert observations into High / Medium / Low

The four scoring dimensions above are the categories. The table below is the operational rubric, what counts as **High**, **Medium**, or **Low** for each. Calibrate the thresholds to your environment, but start from these defaults so two analysts working the same case land at the same score.

<div class="ratm-rubric">
  <div class="ratm-rubric__row ratm-rubric__row--header">
    <span>Dimension</span><span>High</span><span>Medium</span><span>Low</span>
  </div>
  <div class="ratm-rubric__row">
    <span class="ratm-rubric__dim">Asset criticality</span>
    <span>Crown-jewel system; regulated-data store (PCI, PHI, PII); revenue-affecting service.</span>
    <span>Business-critical but not regulated; internal-tooling backbone; broad-access shared service.</span>
    <span>Dev / sandbox / test environment; user-class endpoint with no privileged role; isolated workstation.</span>
  </div>
  <div class="ratm-rubric__row">
    <span class="ratm-rubric__dim">Threat actor intent</span>
    <span>Confirmed targeted activity; TTPs map to a named threat group (APT/ransomware); custom tooling; insider attribution.</span>
    <span>Commodity tooling with some targeting (spear-phish, region/role-specific lure); off-the-shelf C2 framework; financially-motivated opportunist.</span>
    <span>Opportunistic / automated; mass scan; commodity malware on a single endpoint; no targeting signal.</span>
  </div>
  <div class="ratm-rubric__row">
    <span class="ratm-rubric__dim">Exploitation &amp; scope</span>
    <span>Confirmed lateral movement; persistence established; multiple systems compromised; privilege escalation succeeded.</span>
    <span>Single system compromised, no spread confirmed yet; exploitation succeeded but agent contained; credential theft without observed reuse.</span>
    <span>Initial access only / attempted; no execution observed; signature match without behavioral confirmation; exposure without exploitation.</span>
  </div>
  <div class="ratm-rubric__row">
    <span class="ratm-rubric__dim">Business impact</span>
    <span>Regulatory or contractual notification clock triggered (GDPR 72h, HIPAA; PCI DSS via card-brand contracts); revenue-affecting outage; customer-data exposure confirmed.</span>
    <span>Operational disruption possible (team-level); internal-data exposure; brand / customer-trust risk if disclosed.</span>
    <span>Contained, no business effect observed; sandbox-only; visible only to security team.</span>
  </div>
</div>

<Callout variant="info" title="Calibration note">
The thresholds above are starting defaults. Two adjustments most teams make: (1) move *Asset criticality* High to match your actual regulated-asset inventory, not a textbook one, if you have no PCI footprint, "regulated-data store" needs a different anchor; (2) treat *Threat actor intent* High as a *confidence-gated* upgrade, if attribution is speculative, score Medium and note the upgrade path in the RATM record.
</Callout>

<h2 id="mitre">MITRE ATT&CK as a risk lens</h2>

ATT&CK enters Risk as a structuring vocabulary, not a scoring tool. The framework's value at this phase is naming what was observed so the verdict aligns with the broader <DefineTerm term="Threat" display="threat" /> landscape.

<div class="mitre-risk-grid">
  <div class="mitre-risk-card">
    <h4>Tactic-level severity weighting</h4>
    <p>Some tactics carry inherently higher risk than others. Initial Access without follow-on activity is lower risk than confirmed Lateral Movement or Exfiltration. The tactic chain itself shapes priority.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="mitre-risk-card">
    <h4>Technique-to-actor mapping</h4>
    <p>Procedures associated with specific threat actors raise the risk weight. Custom tooling or rare techniques generally indicate targeted activity rather than commodity attacks.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="mitre-risk-card">
    <h4>Coverage gap indicators</h4>
    <p>When the observed chain includes techniques the SOC does not yet have detection for, that gap itself is a risk signal, both for this case and for the program.</p>
  </div>
</div>

<Callout variant="info" title="ATT&CK alone is not the verdict">
Mapping to <DefineTerm term="MITRE ATT&CK" /> is necessary but not sufficient. The framework names what happened; it does not say how much it matters in this environment for this asset. Pair the ATT&CK mapping with the RATM rubric to translate technique-level observations into business-level priority.
</Callout>

<h2 id="cvss">CVSS adaptation for triage</h2>

<DefineTerm term="CVSS">Common Vulnerability Scoring System. A numerical score (0 to 10) describing the severity of a vulnerability across exploitability, impact, and contextual factors. Originally designed for vulnerability management; adapted for active-threat prioritization.</DefineTerm> applies structured methodology to assess risk. By considering attack vector, complexity, and the system's impact on confidentiality, integrity, and availability, CVSS provides a standardized approach to prioritize alerts.

The numerical scoring system (0–10) provides clear thresholds for escalation, with scores grouped into qualitative severity ratings (Low, Medium, High, Critical).

The three metric groups below are the CVSS v3.1 taxonomy, still the most widely deployed in scanners and feeds. CVSS v4.0 (released 2023) reworks the same ideas: the Temporal group becomes Threat metrics, and a Supplemental group is added. The triage guidance on this page applies to both versions.

<div class="cvss-grid">
  <div class="cvss-card cvss-card--base">
    <span class="cvss-card__tag">BASE</span>
    <h4>Base score</h4>
    <p>Reflects the intrinsic severity. Attack vector, privileges required, impact on confidentiality / integrity / availability. A higher score typically indicates a more critical alert.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="cvss-card cvss-card--temporal">
    <span class="cvss-card__tag">TEMPORAL</span>
    <h4>Temporal score</h4>
    <p>Modifies the base score based on current exploitation trends and available mitigation techniques. Adjusts urgency as the threat landscape evolves.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="cvss-card cvss-card--env">
    <span class="cvss-card__tag">ENVIRONMENTAL</span>
    <h4>Environmental score</h4>
    <p>Tailors the score to the organization's infrastructure, business priorities, and existing controls. Evaluates whether critical systems are at risk and whether compensating controls reduce overall exposure.</p>
  </div>
</div>

<Callout variant="warn" title="When CVSS misleads">
A CVSS 9.8 on an asset that is not exposed, not in use, or already mitigated by compensating controls deserves a different response than the same score on a production system. Use CVSS as input, then weight it by the context Subject and Scope provided. The base score is a starting point; the environmental score is what matters for the triage decision.
</Callout>

CVSS is also no longer the only prioritization signal. <strong>EPSS</strong> (Exploit Prediction Scoring System) estimates the probability that a vulnerability will be exploited in the wild within the next 30 days, and <strong>CISA's KEV catalog</strong> lists vulnerabilities with confirmed active exploitation. When a triage case pivots on a vulnerability, KEV membership or a high EPSS score is usually a stronger urgency signal than the CVSS base score alone: a 9.8 nobody exploits matters less this shift than a 7.2 being actively used.

## The triage matrix in practice

The triage matrix is the practical output: impact and likelihood combine into a default action. **Likelihood here means what [Impact + likelihood](/docs/risk/impact-likelihood) defines: confidence that the activity is real and malicious.** The probability that an attack continues or spreads is a separate consideration, scored inside the RATM's exploitation &amp; scope dimension, not smuggled into this axis. Each default action carries the priority band (P1–P4) that the [Escalation criteria](/docs/escalation/criteria) page defines.

<div class="matrix-table">
  <div class="matrix-row matrix-row--header">
    <span>Impact</span><span>Likelihood</span><span>Default action</span>
  </div>
  <div class="matrix-row"><span>High</span><span>High</span><span class="matrix-row__action matrix-row__action--p1">P1 · Escalate immediately</span></div>
  <div class="matrix-row"><span>High</span><span>Medium</span><span class="matrix-row__action matrix-row__action--p2">P2 · Escalate within shift</span></div>
  <div class="matrix-row"><span>High</span><span>Low</span><span class="matrix-row__action matrix-row__action--p3">P3 · Continue investigation; raise priority if evidence strengthens</span></div>
  <div class="matrix-row"><span>Medium</span><span>High</span><span class="matrix-row__action matrix-row__action--p2">P2 · Escalate within shift</span></div>
  <div class="matrix-row"><span>Medium</span><span>Medium</span><span class="matrix-row__action matrix-row__action--p3">P3 · Investigate to closure within 24 hours</span></div>
  <div class="matrix-row"><span>Medium</span><span>Low</span><span class="matrix-row__action matrix-row__action--p4">P4 · Standard queue</span></div>
  <div class="matrix-row"><span>Low</span><span>High</span><span class="matrix-row__action matrix-row__action--p3">P3 · Confirm and close; a real but low-impact finding. Remediate cheaply, tune the control if it keeps firing</span></div>
  <div class="matrix-row"><span>Low</span><span>Medium</span><span class="matrix-row__action matrix-row__action--p4">P4 · Standard queue; close after light investigation</span></div>
  <div class="matrix-row"><span>Low</span><span>Low</span><span class="matrix-row__action matrix-row__action--p5">P4 · Document and close</span></div>
</div>

<Callout variant="info" title="Translate the timing anchors to your team model">
The clock words above are written for a shifted, multi-analyst SOC. On a small or single-person team, translate them without loosening them: "escalate" means engaging whatever your escalation path is (an on-call engineer, an MSSP, an IR retainer), "within shift" means before you stop working the queue today, and "closure within 24 hours" means before the case goes stale. What must survive the translation is the ordering: P1 interrupts whatever you are doing, P2 finishes today, P3 is allowed to wait its turn.
</Callout>

<Callout variant="warn" title="Know the matrix's failure modes">
Qualitative impact × likelihood matrices have documented weaknesses (Cox, 2008): range compression (two Medium/Medium cases can differ by orders of magnitude in real loss), ranking reversals near category boundaries, and anchoring on whichever cell was picked first. The matrix earns its place by making the priority call fast, consistent, and explainable, not by being precise. When a case sits near a boundary or the two axes pull in opposite directions, record the reasoning, not just the cell.
</Callout>

<KeyTakeaway>
The Risk framework produces a verdict the analyst can defend. The RATM rubric does the scoring, ATT&CK names the techniques, and CVSS quantifies <DefineTerm term="Vulnerability" display="vulnerability" /> severity when one is in play. Real triage is four moves: score impact, score likelihood, read the matrix, act on the band. Everything else on this page exists to make those four moves repeatable across analysts.
</KeyTakeaway>
