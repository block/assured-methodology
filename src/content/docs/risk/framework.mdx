## Five moves

Real triage at this phase is five moves. Everything else on this page exists to make them repeatable across analysts.

<ol class="five-moves">
  <li><a href="#ratm">Score impact</a>: if this is real, how bad?</li>
  <li><a href="#ratm">Score likelihood</a> and record confidence beside it. Unknown is not Low.</li>
  <li><a href="#matrix">Read the matrix</a> for the cell and its priority band.</li>
  <li><a href="#overrides">Apply the override rules</a>, and name the one that fired.</li>
  <li><a href="#clocks">Recommend the move for the band</a>. The Escalation decision makes the call.</li>

## One scoring model, two supporting lenses

A structured assessment makes Risk repeatable across analysts and incidents: it reduces variability from individual judgment, creates a shared vocabulary for handoff, and speeds onboarding. Two of the lenses here are industry standards: <DefineTerm term="MITRE ATT&CK" /> and <DefineTerm term="Common Vulnerability Scoring System (CVSS)" display="CVSS" />. The RATM is this methodology's own construct. Adapt its rubric to your industry, <DefineTerm term="Regulatory Requirements" display="regulatory requirements" />, and risk tolerance; what matters is that the whole team scores from the same calibrated rubric, whatever its anchors say.

<span id="other-scoring-schemes"></span>

<Callout variant="info" title="Prior art: how the RATM relates to other scoring schemes">
Scoring incidents on impact and a second, evidence-driven axis is an old idea. The RATM borrows from:

- **NIST SP 800-61r2** (2012, withdrawn in April 2025): section 3.2.6 prioritized incidents by functional impact, information impact and recoverability. The current revision, **NIST SP 800-61r3** (April 2025), keeps the idea as example risk evaluation factors for triage, prioritization and escalation (RS.MA, note N2: asset criticality, functional and data impact, stage of observed activity, threat actor characterization, recoverability).
- **CISA's National Cyber Incident Scoring System (NCISS):** it weights functional and information impact, observed activity, actor characterization and recoverability into a priority level from Baseline to Emergency.
- **NIST SP 800-30 Rev. 1:** the risk-assessment guide combines qualitative likelihood and impact scales in a matrix.
- **ITIL impact × urgency:** the service-desk ancestor of the triage matrix.
- **CISA's SSVC** (Stakeholder-Specific Vulnerability Categorization): a decision tree for vulnerabilities, not alerts. Its questions reappear [below](#cvss) for alerts that name a CVE.

**Splunk Risk-Based Alerting (RBA)** is unrelated despite the similar name: it accumulates risk scores on entities to decide which alerts to raise, while the RATM scores a case after it is raised. What is specific to ASSURED is the evidence discipline around the axes: unknown is not Low, likelihood never rises above the evidence, confidence is recorded beside the cell, and de-escalation is explicit.
</Callout>

<h2 id="ratm">Risk-Based Alert Triage Matrix</h2>

The RATM scores a case across four dimensions and reduces them to the two axes of the triage matrix further down this page. **Asset criticality and business impact make up the impact axis.** **Threat actor intent and exploitation &amp; scope inform the likelihood axis**, which is an estimate, from the evidence, of how probable it is that the activity is malicious or unauthorized. Confidence in that estimate is recorded beside it. Score the dimensions, reduce them to the two axes, read the default action off the matrix.

The reduction is rule-based, so two analysts working the same facts should land on the same cell; regular calibration sessions on shared cases are what make that true in practice:

- **Impact is the higher of asset criticality and business impact.** A crown-jewel system with no business effect observed *yet* still scores High, because impact answers "if this is real, how bad," not "how bad so far." Footprint reaches impact here: asset criticality scores the highest-value asset in confirmed reach, so spread toward a more valuable asset raises the score.
- **Likelihood is an estimate, informed by the evidence and never raised above it.** Threat actor intent and exploitation &amp; scope (did it succeed here?) inform the estimate; sophistication is not a substitute for evidence. The calibration factors on the <a href="/docs/risk/impact-likelihood/">impact + likelihood page</a> may move likelihood by at most one step, and only with the reason written in the case (for example, the observed exploit needs a privilege the account does not hold). They never raise it above what the evidence supports.
- **Confidence is its own line.** Analytic confidence in the likelihood estimate is scored on the <a href="/docs/documentation/standards/#confidence">shared confidence rubric</a> and recorded beside the cell. It does not move the cell.
- **Unknown is not Low.** When the evidence that would decide the question could not be examined (telemetry missing, a source unqueried, a join failed), likelihood cannot read Low: it reads Medium at minimum, confidence reads Low, and the gap is recorded as an open question. A dimension that cannot be assessed scores Medium at minimum on the same rule. "We could not check" never reads the matrix from the same cell as "we checked and it is clean."

### Scoring rubric: how to convert observations into High / Medium / Low

The four scoring dimensions above are the categories. The table below is the operational rubric: what counts as **High**, **Medium**, or **Low** for each. Calibrate the thresholds to your environment, but start from these defaults so two analysts working the same case land at the same score.

<div class="ratm-rubric">
  <div class="ratm-rubric__row ratm-rubric__row--header">
    <span>Dimension</span><span>High</span><span>Medium</span><span>Low</span>
  </div>
  <div class="ratm-rubric__row">
    <span class="ratm-rubric__dim">Asset criticality</span>
    <span>Crown-jewel system; regulated-data store (PCI, PHI, PII); revenue-affecting service. Score the highest-value asset in confirmed reach, not just the entry point: a user-class endpoint whose compromised identity reaches a regulated store scores on the store. Confirmed reach means the identity holds authorized data access to that store, not a mere SSO app assignment; record the access path.</span>
    <span>Business-critical but not regulated; internal-tooling backbone; broad-access shared service.</span>
    <span>Dev / sandbox / test environment; user-class endpoint with no privileged role; isolated workstation.</span>
  </div>
  <div class="ratm-rubric__row">
    <span class="ratm-rubric__dim">Threat actor intent</span>
    <span>Confirmed targeted activity; procedures, tooling or infrastructure attributed to a named threat group with corroboration (<DefineTerm term="Advanced Persistent Threat (APT)" display="APT" />/ransomware); custom tooling; insider attribution.</span>
    <span>Commodity tooling with some targeting (spear-phish, region/role-specific lure); off-the-shelf C2 framework; financially-motivated opportunist.</span>
    <span>Opportunistic / automated; mass scan; commodity malware on a single endpoint; no targeting signal. Exception: an infostealer or loader on a host with live SSO or cloud sessions feeds access brokers, so score exploitation &amp; scope on the credential exposure and treat the identity as compromised.</span>
  </div>
  <div class="ratm-rubric__row">
    <span class="ratm-rubric__dim">Exploitation &amp; scope</span>
    <span>Confirmed lateral movement; persistence established; multiple systems compromised; privilege escalation succeeded.</span>
    <span>Initial access achieved (for example a successful sign-in with stolen credentials) with no follow-on activity yet; single system compromised, no spread confirmed yet; exploitation succeeded but the host was contained (EDR network isolation) before follow-on activity; credential theft without observed reuse.</span>
    <span>Attempted initial access that was blocked or failed; delivery without execution; signature match without behavioral confirmation; exposure without exploitation.</span>
  </div>
  <div class="ratm-rubric__row">
    <span class="ratm-rubric__dim">Business impact</span>
    <span>Regulatory or contractual notification clock triggered (GDPR 72h, HIPAA; PCI DSS via card-brand contracts); revenue-affecting outage; customer-data exposure confirmed; recovery slow or costly (rebuild, restore from backup, data that cannot be recovered).</span>
    <span>Operational disruption possible (team-level); internal-data exposure; brand / customer-trust risk if disclosed; recoverable with planned effort.</span>
    <span>Contained, no business effect observed; sandbox-only; visible only to security team; recovery trivial.</span>
  </div>
</div>

<a id="calibration"></a>**Calibrating the rubric.** The thresholds above are starting defaults. Most teams make two adjustments:

1. Anchor *asset criticality* High to your actual regulated-asset inventory, not a textbook one. If you have no PCI footprint, "regulated-data store" needs a different anchor.
2. Treat *threat actor intent* High as a confidence-gated upgrade: if attribution is speculative, score Medium and record the upgrade path in the RATM record.

Then check that the rubric holds in practice: have two analysts score the same sampled cases each month, track how often they land on the same cell, and talk through every disagreement.

<h2 id="matrix">The triage matrix in practice</h2>

The triage matrix is the practical output: impact and likelihood combine into a priority band and a recommended move. **Likelihood here means what [Impact + likelihood](/docs/risk/impact-likelihood/) defines: how probable it is, given the evidence, that the activity is malicious or unauthorized.** Confidence is recorded beside the cell. Whether an attack will continue or spread is not a likelihood question: footprint reaches the matrix through impact (the confirmed-reach rule) and through the lateral-movement escalation criterion. This page owns the band definitions: the cell-to-band mapping, the override rules and the [default clocks](#clocks) for each band. The [priority levels](/docs/escalation/criteria/#priority-levels-p1-to-p4) on the Escalation criteria page explain only what each band means for the close-or-escalate decision.

<Callout variant="warn" title="Live activity sets the urgency">
The matrix cannot tell a live intrusion from a dormant one that happens to share its cells. It does not need to: confirmed malicious activity that no control has fully contained (an attacker session still open, exfiltration still running, persistence still in place) already meets escalation criterion 1, so the escalation tripwire below makes it P1 whatever the cell. What Risk adds is the time-sensitivity in the recommendation. Every minute before a stolen session is revoked is a minute of live access, so the record says what to cut first.
</Callout>

<h3 id="overrides">The override rules: when the matrix is not the last word</h3>

The matrix cell is the **default** action. Exactly three overrides can modify it, applied in this order, and the case record names the rule that fired. These are the same rules the <a href="/docs/escalation/criteria/">Escalation criteria</a> page enforces; there is one contract, stated here, consumed there.

<div class="override-list">
  <div class="override-item">
    <h4>1 · Escalation tripwire, up to P1, from any phase</h4>
    <p>Any escalation criterion <strong>confirmed</strong> makes the case P1 immediately, whatever the cell and whichever phase the case is in: confirmed malicious activity not fully contained by a control, confirmed malicious or unauthorized activity touching a business-critical asset, confirmed lateral movement or exfiltration, or a confirmed active campaign. Confirmed means the claim reaches High on the shared <a href="/docs/documentation/standards/#confidence">confidence rubric</a>; below that it is a suspicion, and suspicion refines Scope. Confirmation means evidence, not asset value: a crown-jewel alert with nothing validated yet has not tripped the wire.</p>
    <p><strong>Contained by a control:</strong> activity a control blocked or quarantined, with no execution or follow-on evidence, does not page IR. Verify that the control held (the block or quarantine event, no process from the artifact, no callback), record the case at P3 or P4, and propose the pattern as a candidate pattern entry.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="override-item">
    <h4>2 · The high-impact clock, tightening P3</h4>
    <p>A High-impact case never idles on uncertainty. If likelihood is genuinely Low because the evidence was examined and leans benign, the High/Low cell stands at P3, but on a tightened clock: re-score within 4 hours by default (calibrate to your staffing, but keep it well under the standard 24), with named upgrade triggers and the queue lead or asset owner aware the case exists. If likelihood is "Low" only because evidence is missing or unobtainable, that is Unknown: likelihood reads Medium at minimum, confidence reads Low, and the case reads High/Medium: P2, escalate within 4 hours.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="override-item">
    <h4>3 · De-escalation is explicit</h4>
    <p>A priority moves down only with a named reason recorded in the case and the same stakeholder notification the upgrade would get. Silent decay is how a P2 becomes nobody's problem. A confirmed criterion is not the triage analyst's to downgrade: only the receiving owner can, after receipt.</p>
  </div>
</div>

Run the hardest case through the contract to see it hold: a domain controller alert with weak, incomplete evidence. Impact is High (asset criticality). The evidence is missing, not benign-leaning, so likelihood reads Medium with confidence Low; the cell is High/Medium and the action is P2, escalate within 4 hours, on every page of this site. If instead the evidence is present and points benign, it is High/Low: P3 on the tightened clock. Neither branch waits 24 hours, and neither branch pages IR at 3 a.m. on asset value alone.

<h3 id="clocks">Default clocks for each band</h3>

<EscalationRule show={["clocks"]} caption="Acknowledge and decision clocks by band" />

The clocks are written for a shifted, multi-analyst SOC. On a small or [single-person team](/docs/operating-variants/#the-one-person-soc), translate them without loosening them: "escalate" means engaging whatever your escalation path is (an on-call engineer, an MSSP, an IR retainer), "within 4 hours" still means hours, not tomorrow, and "within 24 hours" means before the case goes stale. What must survive the translation is the ordering: P1 interrupts whatever you are doing, P2 moves within hours, P3 waits its turn against a named trigger.

    const asset = field("asset").value;
    const business = field("business").value;
    const intent = field("intent").value;
    const exploitation = field("exploitation").value;
    const unknown = field("unknown").checked;
    const criterion = field("criterion").checked;
    const containedTicked = field("contained").checked;
    let confidence = field("confidence").value;
    const notes = [];

    const contained = containedTicked && !unknown;
    if (containedTicked && unknown) {
      notes.push("Contained by a control does not apply: a control cannot be shown to have held when the evidence could not be examined.");
    }

    const impact = higher(asset, business);
    let likelihood = exploitation;
    let why = exploitation + ", from exploitation & scope.";
    if (contained) {
      likelihood = "Low";
      why = "Low: the control held, with no execution or follow-on evidence.";
    } else if (rank(intent) > rank(exploitation)) {
      likelihood = R[rank(exploitation) + 1];
      why = likelihood + ": exploitation & scope reads " + exploitation + ", and the higher intent score raises it one step, with the reason written in the case.";
    } else if (rank(intent) < rank(exploitation)) {
      why = exploitation + ", from exploitation & scope. The lower intent score does not drag it down: intent speaks to who and why.";
    }
    if (unknown) {
      if (rank(likelihood) < rank("Medium")) likelihood = "Medium";
      confidence = "Low";
      why = likelihood + ": unknown is not Low, so likelihood reads Medium at minimum.";
      notes.push("Unknown is not Low: likelihood reads Medium at minimum, confidence reads Low, and the gap is recorded as an open question.");

    const cell = rules.cells.find((c) => c.impact === impact && c.likelihood === likelihood);
    if (!cell) return;

    const band = tripwire ? "P1" : cell.band;

    if (tripwire) {
      notes.unshift("Escalation tripwire: a criterion is confirmed, so the case is P1 whatever the cell, from any phase.");
    } else if (criterion && !contained) {
      notes.push("No tripwire: the criterion is not confirmed at " + confidence + " confidence. " + rules.confirmed);
    }
    if (contained) notes.push("Contained by a control. " + rules.contained);

    const highImpactClock = !tripwire && impact === "High" && likelihood === "Low";
    if (highImpactClock) {
      notes.push("High-impact clock: the High/Low cell stands at P3, but re-scores within " + rules.highImpactRescoreHours + " hours, not " + rules.p3RescoreHours + ", with named upgrade triggers and the queue lead or asset owner aware the case exists.");
    }

    const clock = rules.clocks.find((c) => c.band === band);
    if (!clock) return;
    const decision = highImpactClock
      ? "Re-score within " + rules.highImpactRescoreHours + " hours on a named trigger, then close or escalate"
      : clock.decisionFull;

    out("impact", impact + ": the higher of asset criticality (" + asset + ") and business impact (" + business + ").");

    out("confidence", confidence + (unknown ? ": the unexamined evidence holds it at Low." : ", recorded beside the cell. It does not move the cell."));

})();
`}</script>

<h3 id="identity-containment">Recommending identity containment</h3>

For a compromised identity, the recommended move names the order, because the instinctive step is the wrong one. Whether a password reset ends existing sessions depends on the identity provider and how the reset is done, and it never removes an OAuth consent grant or an MFA method the attacker registered. Preserve before you contain, cut access with reversible actions, then verify:

1. **Preserve:** the sign-in and session records, the relevant audit logs, mailbox rules, app grants, and a snapshot of the account's MFA methods and devices.
2. **Contain, reversibly (pre-authorized):** block sign-in, revoke all sessions and refresh tokens, disable sign-in for any app the session consented to, and disable hiding or forwarding rules. A session that a downstream platform already issued behind SSO needs that platform's owner to end it there.
3. **Verify:** the attacker's session stops presenting. Access tokens already issued can stay valid for an hour or more (a random 60 to 90 minutes by default on Microsoft Entra ID) where continuous access evaluation is not enforced, so check, do not assume.
4. **With IR or owner approval:** reset the password, remove the app's grants, and remove MFA methods or devices the user did not register. These cannot be undone, so they are not on the pre-authorized list unless the protocol names them for the scenario.

Any user who reached the credential step of a phishing kit is credential-exposed until the password is reset, sessions are revoked and MFA methods are reviewed. The [identity-persistence sweep](/docs/reference/identity-sweep/) lists the eight places an attacker leaves a way back in (MFA methods, registered devices, app grants, mailbox rules and more) and where each is logged. [Case C](/docs/risk/example/#case-c) runs this order with its timestamps.

<h3 id="entity">Score the case, and watch the entity</h3>

The case, not the alert, is the unit of work. Alerts on the same entity and window, or under one vendor incident ID, form one case, and the riskiest member sets its level (see [correlated cases](/docs/fast-path/#correlated-cases)). Across cases, watch the entity too. Several P4 closes on the same identity or host within days are a signal in their own right, and slow attacks live in exactly that gap. A practical default: three Low/Low closes on the same account in seven days trigger a re-score of the entity's recent activity as one case before the next alert on it closes alone, with the earlier cases it folds in named in the record. The fast path applies the same idea at Level 0: three pattern closes on one entity in seven days force a Level 1 review.

<h3 id="undetermined">When the activity verdict stays undetermined</h3>

Some cases end the loop without an answer: the vendor's logs have not arrived, the owner has not replied, the one source that would decide it is out of retention. Undetermined is a legitimate activity verdict, but it is not a benign close and not a place to park a case. Score it like any other case, with the evidence rules applied:

- **Score the gap, not a guess.** Likelihood reads Medium at minimum and confidence reads Low, because unknown is not Low. The cell, and so the band and its clock, follow from that: a High-impact undetermined case is P2.
- **Name the gap and the re-score trigger.** Record which question is open, what evidence would answer it, and a named trigger with a time: the log delivery, the end of a forward watch, the owner's answer, or a fixed re-score time when nothing better exists.
- **At the trigger, re-score.** If the new evidence decides it, the case reads its new cell. If it is still undetermined, the Escalation decision applies the E rule to the band: escalate at P1 or P2; at P3 or P4, a documented close is allowed only with the gaps named and a reopen trigger written, or the case moves up a level of depth.

Undetermined is never closed silently, and never held open without a named trigger. [Case D](/docs/cases/case-d/) runs this rule end to end.

<Callout variant="warn" title="Know the matrix's failure modes">
Qualitative impact × likelihood matrices have documented weaknesses (Cox, "What's Wrong with Risk Matrices?", *Risk Analysis* 28(2), 2008, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1539-6924.2008.01030.x">doi:10.1111/j.1539-6924.2008.01030.x</a>): range compression (two Medium/Medium cases can differ by orders of magnitude in real loss), ranking errors near category boundaries, and ambiguous inputs that let different analysts rate the same case differently. Anchoring on the first cell picked is a separate cognitive trap (see <a href="/docs/cognitive-traps/">Thinking traps</a>). The matrix earns its place by making the priority call fast, consistent, and explainable, not by being precise. When a case sits near a boundary or the two axes pull in opposite directions, record the reasoning, not just the cell.
</Callout>

<h2 id="mitre">MITRE ATT&CK as a risk lens</h2>

ATT&CK enters Risk as a structuring vocabulary, not a scoring tool. Mapping to <DefineTerm term="MITRE ATT&CK" /> names what was observed, so the assessment aligns with the broader <DefineTerm term="Threat" display="threat" /> landscape; it does not say how much the activity matters in this environment for this asset. Pair the mapping with the RATM rubric to turn technique-level observations into a business-level priority, and record the ATT&CK version you mapped against.

<div class="mitre-risk-grid">
  <div class="mitre-risk-card">
    <h3>Tactic-level stage context</h3>
    <p>How far the <em>confirmed</em> chain has progressed shapes priority: Initial Access without follow-on activity is a smaller case than confirmed Lateral Movement or Exfiltration. The stage must be evidenced, not inferred from a technique's name; a tactic label alone never raises the score.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="mitre-risk-card">
    <h3>Technique-to-actor mapping</h3>
    <p>Procedures, tooling and infrastructure attributed to a specific actor, with corroboration, can raise threat actor intent; overlap on common techniques alone cannot, because widely used techniques appear across dozens of groups. Custom tooling or rare procedures suggest targeted activity rather than commodity attacks.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="mitre-risk-card">
    <h3>Coverage gap indicators</h3>
    <p>When the observed chain includes techniques the SOC does not yet have detection for, that gap itself is a risk signal, both for this case and for the program.</p>
  </div>
</div>

<h2 id="cvss">Vulnerability signals: CVSS, KEV, EPSS and SSVC</h2>

<DefineTerm term="Common Vulnerability Scoring System (CVSS)" display="CVSS" /> scores the technical severity of a vulnerability, not the risk of an alert that involves it, and FIRST, which maintains it, says it should not be used alone to assess risk. Scanners and feeds carry a mix of v3.1 and <a href="https://www.first.org/cvss/v4.0/specification-document">v4.0</a> vectors; a v4.0 score should say which metric groups it uses (CVSS-B, CVSS-BT, CVSS-BE, CVSS-BTE), and a bare CVSS-B score is worst-case severity with no threat or environmental context. Expect gaps in the score itself, too: since April 2026 the National Vulnerability Database (<DefineTerm term="NVD / EUVD" display="NVD" />) no longer routinely adds its own CVSS score where the CVE Numbering Authority (CNA) that published the record supplied one, and many lower-priority CVEs are not enriched at all, so a feed may carry a CNA-only score, mixed v3.1 and v4.0 vectors, or no score.

Read the score beside three exploitation signals: membership in CISA's <DefineTerm term="Known Exploited Vulnerabilities (KEV) Catalog" display="Known Exploited Vulnerabilities (KEV)" /> catalog (<a href="https://www.cisa.gov/known-exploited-vulnerabilities-catalog">CISA</a>), the <DefineTerm term="Exploit Prediction Scoring System (EPSS)" display="EPSS" /> (<a href="https://www.first.org/epss/">FIRST</a>, v4) estimate of the probability of exploitation in the next 30 days, and the <a href="https://certcc.github.io/SSVC/">SSVC</a> decision points (exploitation, exposure, automatability, technical impact) that CISA's <a href="https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/directives/bod-26-04-prioritizing-security-updates-based-risk">BOD 26-04</a> (June 2026) now uses to set federal remediation timelines; it supersedes BOD 22-01 and BOD 19-02. KEV membership or a high EPSS score usually outranks the base score: a 9.8 nobody exploits matters less today than a 7.2 being actively used.

Know EPSS's limits: it is not a risk score, it scores only published CVEs, it knows nothing about your assets, exposure or compensating controls, and NIST's <a href="https://doi.org/10.6028/NIST.CSWP.41">CSWP 41</a> (2025) notes it underestimates vulnerabilities already observed being exploited. Check KEV first, then EPSS. Absence from KEV is not evidence of safety, though: unknown is not Low.

For an alert that names a CVE, the decisive questions are about this target, not the score:

1. **Is the target vulnerable?** It runs an affected version and configuration.
2. **Is it reachable?** The vulnerable surface is exposed to where the attempt came from.
3. **Is the CVE in KEV, or high on EPSS?**
4. **Did the attempt succeed?** Execution, a new process, an outbound callback.

Score exploitation &amp; scope from question 4, not from the CVSS score. An intrusion detection system (IDS) hit for a CVE against a patched host is an attempt, scored Low, whatever its base score; questions 2 and 3 set how fast the patch or block should follow elsewhere.

<KeyTakeaway>
The priority matrix produces a score and a recommendation the analyst can defend. The RATM rubric does the scoring, ATT&CK names the techniques, and CVSS, KEV and EPSS inform exploitation &amp; scope when a <DefineTerm term="Vulnerability" display="vulnerability" /> is in play. Real triage is five moves: score impact, score likelihood and record confidence (unknown is not Low), read the matrix, apply the override rules, recommend the move for the band; the Escalation decision makes the call. A severity score that arrives with the alert, from a vendor console or an AI assistant, is one more input to score, not an anchor (see <a href="/docs/cognitive-traps/">Thinking traps</a> and <a href="/docs/ai-triage/">AI-assisted triage</a>).
</KeyTakeaway>
