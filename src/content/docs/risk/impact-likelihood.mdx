# Evaluating impact

Impact is "if this is real, how bad is it?" The dimension has four sub-questions:

<div class="impact-list">
  <div class="il"><h4>🏢 Operational impact</h4><p>What services would be degraded or unavailable? For how long? How many users affected?</p></div>
  <div class="il"><h4>💰 Financial impact</h4><p>Direct cost (response, remediation, downtime) and indirect (lost revenue, contract penalties, fraud).</p></div>
  <div class="il"><h4>📋 Regulatory impact</h4><p>Notifications required, possible fines, audit consequences. Already framed during Scope.</p></div>
  <div class="il"><h4>🪞 Reputational impact</h4><p>Customer trust, partner relationships, public reporting. Hard to quantify; still real.</p></div>
</div>

# Evaluating likelihood

Likelihood is "how confident are we that this finding is real and the evidence supports it?" The dimension is about the strength of the evidence, not the severity of the threat.

<div class="impact-list">
  <div class="il"><h4>🔬 Evidence quality</h4><p>How many independent sources confirm the finding? How clean is each one?</p></div>
  <div class="il"><h4>🧩 Pattern fit</h4><p>How well does the evidence match a known attack pattern? Strong fit raises confidence; partial fit lowers it.</p></div>
  <div class="il"><h4>👻 Alternative explanations</h4><p>Can the evidence be explained by legitimate activity? If yes, with what likelihood? Until those are ruled out, the likelihood is not high.</p></div>
  <div class="il"><h4>🌐 Corroborating intelligence</h4><p>Does external threat intelligence describe this pattern? Corroboration raises likelihood; absence does not lower it on its own.</p></div>
</div>

<Callout variant="info" title="Separating the two">
A common mistake is letting impact drive likelihood. "This would be really bad, therefore it is probably real." That is backwards. Evaluate likelihood from the evidence, impact from the potential consequences, and then combine them deliberately.
</Callout>

# Producing the verdict

The Risk output should include:

- **Impact score** (high / medium / low), with a one-sentence rationale.
- **Likelihood score** (high / medium / low), with a one-sentence rationale.
- **Combined priority** from the triage matrix.
- **Open questions** the analyst could not resolve, and what would change the verdict.

That last item is what makes the verdict defensible. Recording open questions is the difference between "the analyst was confident" and "the analyst knew what they did not know."

<KeyTakeaway>Impact and likelihood are independent. Combine them deliberately. Record the rationale for each. The verdict is not a number; it is a defended judgment.</KeyTakeaway>
