## The dynamic loop: Scope → Uncover → Risk

Triage is not strictly linear. The investigative engine of ASSURED is an iterative loop through Scope, Uncover, and Risk that runs until clarity is reached. Risk findings often send the analyst back to Uncover for more evidence; new evidence sometimes forces a fresh Scope decision; that fresh Scope reframes what Uncover and Risk look at next. Embracing the loop is part of the methodology.

<div class="loop-grid">
  <div class="loop-card">
    <span class="loop-card__num">01</span>
    <h3>Initial Scope</h3>
    <p>Define investigation boundaries from the alert context. Deliberately narrow at the outset to keep noise down.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="loop-card">
    <span class="loop-card__num">02</span>
    <h3>Uncover evidence</h3>
    <p>Gather and analyze data from targeted sources. Bounded by the current scope to avoid sprawl.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="loop-card">
    <span class="loop-card__num">03</span>
    <h3>Assess risk</h3>
    <p>Evaluate impact and likelihood based on what was found. The triage matrix sets the priority (P1-P4) and its clock, and Risk recommends one of three moves: close, escalate, or rescope and run the loop again. The Escalation decision records the call.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="loop-card">
    <span class="loop-card__num">04</span>
    <h3>Refine scope</h3>
    <p>Adjust boundaries based on new understanding. Reactivates the loop with updated parameters.</p>
  </div>
</div>

<Callout variant="info" title="Triggers that send you back through the loop">
The loop iterates when something surfaces the original scope did not anticipate: signs of lateral movement into unscoped systems, use of alternate user identities or service accounts, possible data staging or exfiltration, or discovery of a high-value or compliance-sensitive asset that was not in the original entity map. Each pass increases investigative fidelity. For unresolved cases the loop terminates when the residual risk is sufficiently understood to justify a resolution: close or escalate.

One exception outranks the loop: a *confirmed* escalation criterion trips the [escalation tripwire](/docs/escalation/criteria/#tripwire), and the case escalates from wherever the loop stands (see the [override rules](/docs/risk/framework/#overrides)).
</Callout>

## Why Risk is its own phase

- **Evidence and impact are separate questions.** Uncover answers what happened. Risk answers how much it matters. Skipping Risk means escalating every finding equally, which is the same as not prioritizing at all.
- **Likelihood and confidence are separate lines.** Likelihood estimates how probable it is that the activity is malicious; confidence says how well the evidence supports that estimate. A high-impact finding resting on thin evidence is a different decision from the same finding with airtight evidence, so the analyst states both.
- **Context changes the priority.** The same intrusion on a sandbox is not the same intrusion on a regulated production system. Risk is the phase that brings that context into the priority.
- **Benign closes have value.** A benign close sharpens detection, whether the rule misfired or fired correctly on authorized activity. The methodology asks the analyst to capture what they learned, even when the verdict is "no incident."

<KeyTakeaway>Risk turns evidence into priority and records confidence beside it. Its output is a scored recommendation the Escalation decision acts on, with reasoning the rest of the team can defend.</KeyTakeaway>
