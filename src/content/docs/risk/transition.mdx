<Callout variant="info" title="Five phases complete. The next begins.">
Alert, Subject, Scope, Uncover, and Risk together produced the picture and the verdict. Escalation now elevates the event beyond triage when the cumulative findings indicate the activity exceeds predefined thresholds of acceptable risk.
</Callout>

## The loop ends here, or it doesn't

The Scope → Uncover → Risk loop introduced on the [Risk overview](/docs/risk) is the engine that drove the investigation to this point. For an *unresolved* case, crossing into Escalation happens when the loop terminates, when the residual risk is well-enough understood to justify a resolution decision. If the evidence is ambiguous and the loop is still sharpening the picture, refine the scope and run it again rather than escalating a case you cannot yet defend.

The loop is never a reason to sit on a confirmed criterion, though. A loop that is *confirming* new <DefineTerm term="Lateral Movement" display="lateral movement" /> or <DefineTerm term="Data Staging" display="data staging" /> has already answered the escalation question: the [break-glass rule](/docs/escalation/criteria) fires, IR takes ownership, and the loop keeps running in parallel as scoping support under IR's clock. The distinction is confirmation versus suspicion. Suspicion iterates; confirmation escalates from wherever the investigation happens to be standing.

---

## When escalation is warranted

Escalation is warranted when the cumulative findings indicate that observed activity exceeds predefined thresholds of acceptable risk. Crucially, escalation is not reactionary. It is a deliberate step grounded in collected evidence and organizational policy.

<div class="escalate-grid">
  <div class="escalate-card">
    <h4>⚠️ Confirmed malicious behavior</h4>
    <p>Evidence supports compromise. Indicators map cleanly to known tactics or campaigns. The verdict is high-likelihood, and the activity is ongoing or has produced material impact.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="escalate-card">
    <h4>💎 High-value asset involvement</h4>
    <p>Primary entities include crown-jewel infrastructure, regulated data stores, or systems supporting critical operations. The asset profile elevates the response posture independent of the technical evidence's depth.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="escalate-card">
    <h4>📋 Regulatory exposure</h4>
    <p>Evidence triggers reportable incident thresholds under GDPR, HIPAA, PCI DSS, or the SEC disclosure rule, or touches SOX-scoped financial systems. The compliance clocks run independently of the technical investigation; escalation lets compliance engage on the parallel track.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="escalate-card">
    <h4>📌 Persistence indicators</h4>
    <p>The adversary has established or is establishing mechanisms for long-term access. Containment requires response capabilities beyond what triage provides.</p>
  </div>
</div>

## What an actionable escalation includes

Risk's handoff to Escalation must be both justified and actionable.

<div class="actionable-grid">
  <div class="actionable-card">
    <span class="actionable-card__num">01</span>
    <h4>Articulate why the risk is unacceptable</h4>
    <p>Based on observed evidence. Not "this seems bad" but "impact X, likelihood Y, here are the named inputs that produced both."</p>
  </div>
  <div class="actionable-card">
    <span class="actionable-card__num">02</span>
    <h4>Identify affected systems, users, or data</h4>
    <p>With precision. Primary entities, secondary entities, downstream exposures. The escalation team should not have to re-derive the entity map.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="actionable-card">
    <span class="actionable-card__num">03</span>
    <h4>Outline containment needs and timing</h4>
    <p>What needs to happen now vs. what can wait. Time-sensitive factors (active session, ongoing exfiltration, regulatory clock) get called out explicitly.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="actionable-card">
    <span class="actionable-card__num">04</span>
    <h4>Curate the supporting data</h4>
    <p>Validated, deduplicated, ready for transfer. Logs, timeline, ATT&CK mapping, intel context. The next analyst should not have to re-pull the same data.</p>
  </div>
</div>

<Callout variant="info" title="Escalation imposes a communication obligation">
Analysts must present the case in a way that is immediately understandable to responders. Relevant observables, supporting context, current status, and recommended next steps, conveyed through structured documentation or templated workflows. The form is as important as the content.
</Callout>

---

<KeyTakeaway>
The decision to escalate reflects not just risk severity, but investigative completeness. If the evidence shows material risk that cannot be mitigated within the triage process, escalation becomes the formal point of transition. From this point forward, the event moves from isolated analysis to collaborative action, guided by organizational response frameworks.
</KeyTakeaway>
