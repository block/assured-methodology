<Callout variant="info" title="What Scope is for">
Subject produced a map of identities and relationships, with a provisional primary, secondary and downstream classification. Scope takes that map and draws the formal boundary: which regulations constrain the work, which time windows apply, which entities are in this investigation, and which <DefineTerm term="Infrastructure" display="infrastructure" /> paths get walked. Without scope, Uncover has no idea where to look.

Regulatory comes first not because it is most important, but because its clocks may already be running: tag it from Subject's map now, and refine it as the entity boundary firms up. The boundary is not fixed once drawn either. When Uncover or Risk surfaces a new entity, a new window, or a new regulated data class, Scope redraws it, in both directions; that loop is the method working.
</Callout>

## Scope at a glance

<PhaseAtAGlance phase="scope">
  <Fragment slot="deliverable">The boundary statement: one page covering the threat type, the windows, the entities in and out, regulatory tags with their candidate timestamps, visibility gaps, preservation, open questions and a time box. Start from the [Scope boundary template](/templates/scope-boundary.md).</Fragment>
  <Fragment slot="done">Every entity in the boundary has a category, a depth and a one-line justification. Each window has an anchor event and a retention check. Every regulatory tag has its candidate timestamps and an owner engaged. Every gap is named. Uncover has its questions and its time box.</Fragment>
  <Fragment slot="level-0">No separate Scope. The eligibility gate's items (asset criticality, privilege, regulated-data reach), each proven from its source system, stand in for it. An item that fails or cannot be proven ends Level 0.</Fragment>
  <Fragment slot="level-1">Subject and Scope run as one sweep, and Scope is the three-line minimum below.</Fragment>
  <Fragment slot="level-2">The full boundary statement across all four boundary types, versioned each time Uncover or Risk sends the case back.</Fragment>
</PhaseAtAGlance>

<Callout variant="info" title="Minimum viable Scope">
A Level 1 case gets three lines instead of a page: the window and its anchor; the subject and what is excluded; any regulatory flag (or a documented "none") and any named gap. If any of those will not fit on one line, the case has outgrown Level 1. Regulated data merely in reach is a Scope flag. Regulated data plausibly accessed by the activity under review is what moves a case to Level 2.
</Callout>

## Why Scope is its own phase

- **Without scope, investigations sprawl.** Analysts who skip Scope follow every interesting thread. Each one feels productive; by the end of the shift three are open, none is resolved, and the alert that started it is still waiting for a decision.
- **Compliance lives at this step.** An account that touched HIPAA-bound data carries different obligations than a developer's sandbox. Scope recognizes those obligations before Uncover starts touching data that has rules attached.
- **Time is a boundary, not a default.** A fast-moving intrusion may need a day of lookback; a slow-burn insider case may need six months. The window is a choice about the threat, not a SIEM setting.
- **Visibility is finite.** Air-gapped systems, SaaS without logging access, off-network devices: naming the gaps up front keeps Uncover from spending time on queries that cannot be answered.

<KeyTakeaway>
Scope is the phase that says "this and not that." It covers four boundary types (regulatory, time, entity, infrastructure) and produces one page of output: the clocks, windows, entities, and visibility limits that every later phase works within.
</KeyTakeaway>
