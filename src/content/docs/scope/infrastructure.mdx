# Four common boundary types

<div class="infra-grid">
  <div class="infra-card">
    <h4>🌐 Network segmentation</h4>
    <p>Different network segments have different visibility. A DMZ host's traffic is captured at the perimeter; an internal segment may only have summary NetFlow. Scope acknowledges which segments are in and which are off-instrument.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="infra-card">
    <h4>☁️ Cloud account boundaries</h4>
    <p>Multi-account or multi-cloud environments often have inconsistent logging coverage. Logs that exist in one account may be unavailable in another. Federated identity makes the boundary even harder.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="infra-card">
    <h4>🌍 Geographic boundaries</h4>
    <p>Data sovereignty rules can prevent moving log data across regions. An investigation may need to be conducted in-region or by a regional team.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="infra-card">
    <h4>📅 Retention windows</h4>
    <p>How long each data source is kept. Differs by source (firewall logs may be 30 days, identity logs 1 year). Older evidence may not exist at all.</p>
  </div>
</div>

# Naming what is invisible

A useful Scope output is an explicit list of what the SOC cannot see for this case:

- **Off-network mobile devices.** Activity that happens when the device is off-corporate-network may be invisible until the device reconnects.
- **Personal devices in BYOD environments.** Often only see authentication events, not deeper telemetry.
- **Third-party SaaS without integrated logging.** The SOC sees the authentication; the in-app activity is in the vendor's logs and may not be accessible without a vendor request.
- **Air-gapped systems.** May produce no telemetry that reaches the SIEM at all.
- **Encrypted traffic without decryption.** TLS metadata is visible; payload content is not.

<Callout variant="info" title="Naming gaps is part of scope">
Listing what cannot be seen is not an apology. It is a deliberate statement that frames the investigation realistically. Uncover then spends time on questions the available telemetry can actually answer.
</Callout>

<KeyTakeaway>
Infrastructure boundaries are the honest map of the SOC's vision. Investigations that respect those boundaries finish on time and on point. Investigations that ignore them waste days on queries the data could never have answered.
</KeyTakeaway>
