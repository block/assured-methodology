## What Scope hands forward

A one-page boundary statement. Start from the <a href="/templates/scope-boundary.md" download>scope-boundary.md</a> template; Case A's is filled in on the [worked example](/docs/scope/example/#case-a). It answers eight questions, so an analyst joining mid-case knows what is in, what is out, and what Uncover is looking for:

1. **Threat type:** which playbooks and data sources Uncover reaches for.
2. **Windows:** primary, extended and forward, in UTC, each with its anchor event and a retention check.
3. **Entities and sweeps:** primary at full depth, secondary at baseline, downstream and originating threat placed, sweeps as bounded queries, and what is out, each with its reason.
4. **Constraints:** regulatory tags with candidate timestamps and the time each owner was engaged; named visibility gaps (a source the SOC cannot see is a gap, not an out-of-scope decision); requests in flight.
5. **Open questions** for Uncover (below).
6. **Escalation posture:** containment status and the findings that would fire the <a href="/docs/escalation/criteria/">escalation tripwire</a>. If it has fired, say so: IR owns the case.
7. **Preservation:** what is held, hashed or paused, so Uncover knows what still exists.
8. **Time box:** when Uncover converges, re-scopes, or pulls in a peer or tiers the case up (escalation to IR runs on the criteria and the matrix, not on elapsed time), sized from the <a href="/docs/scope/time/">time guidance</a>.

## Depth and the close path

Scope never closes a case; the Escalation decision does, and a confirmed criterion is an early E decision from here as from any phase. A narrow boundary is what makes a fast, defensible close possible (Case B). Level 0 has no Scope step: the eligibility gate stands in for it. Level 1 compresses Scope to three lines inside the context sweep: the window and its anchor; the subject and what is excluded; any regulatory flag and named gap. Level 2 gets the full statement. The [depth ladder](/docs/fast-path/) only moves up.

## Framing the Uncover questions

Uncover's depth comes from specific questions, not broad queries: initial access; extent of compromise; persistence (including OAuth consent grants, mailbox rules, new MFA methods or devices, IDE extensions and MCP servers); lateral movement; data movement; and intent. Each open question in the statement should map to a query Uncover can run.

## A good handoff vs a bad one

**Bad:** "Investigate everything around dlin's laptop. PCI may apply." **Good:** Case A's [boundary statement](/docs/scope/example/#case-a), with its most expensive line corrected. It records that the page had not gone out; it should read "escalation tripwire met at 09:24 (criterion 1); IR paged and owns the case; Uncover runs as scoping support."

## When Uncover hits the line

Stop at the boundary, write the three-line justification (evidence, risk, required actions: the <a href="/docs/scope/entity/#scope-evolution-the-decision-tree">entity decision tree</a>), and extend the statement with a timestamp. Clearing works the same way: an entity the evidence clears leaves with its reason recorded. A confirmed criterion fires the escalation tripwire; suspicion only refines Scope.

## You are ready for Uncover if

- Another analyst could run Uncover's first query from the statement alone: every entity in or out with a reason, every open question mapped to a query.
- Every gap, hold, request and regulatory timestamp has a time on it, and the escalation posture says who owns the case.

<KeyTakeaway>
Scope is invisible when it works: Uncover starts inside the boundary and finishes on time. The line moves only with a recorded justification, and a crossing that confirms an escalation criterion makes it IR's case.
</KeyTakeaway>
