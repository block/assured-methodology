# Risk vs. threat

The vocabulary matters. The two words mean different things, and the response differs accordingly.

<div class="insider-distinction">
  <div class="insider-card insider-card--risk">
    <h3>🟡 Insider risk</h3>
    <p>The possibility that an insider's actions (intentional or not) cause harm. Includes mistakes, policy violations, unsafe habits, and well-meaning workarounds. Most insider risk is not malicious. Most insider risk is also not random; it follows the same patterns of stress, change, and incentive that any human behavior does.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="insider-card insider-card--threat">
    <h3>🔴 Insider threat</h3>
    <p>An insider acting with intent to harm the organization. Theft of intellectual property, sabotage, fraud, exfiltration to an external party. Far rarer than risk, but with disproportionate impact when it happens. The methodology for detecting threat overlaps with risk monitoring but requires escalation patterns that the rest of the SOC may not be used to.</p>
  </div>
</div>

<Callout variant="info" title="Why the distinction matters">
Treating all insider activity as threat poisons the relationship with the rest of the organization and produces investigations that find nothing useful. Treating all insider activity as risk misses the rare but consequential intentional cases. The methodology asks the analyst to start with risk and escalate to threat only when the evidence supports it.
</Callout>

# The Insider Threat Matrix

The <DefineTerm term="Insider Threat Matrix">A community-maintained framework at insiderthreatmatrix.org. Catalogs insider techniques across phases (motive, means, preparation, infringement, anti-forensics) similar to how MITRE ATT&CK catalogs external adversary techniques. The matrix provides shared vocabulary and detection guidance.</DefineTerm> at <a href="https://insiderthreatmatrix.org" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">insiderthreatmatrix.org</a> organizes insider tradecraft into five phases. Used as a lens, it gives analysts a vocabulary for describing what they are seeing.

<div class="matrix-phases">
  <div class="matrix-phase">
    <span class="matrix-phase__num">01</span>
    <h4>🎯 Motivation</h4>
    <p>Why an insider might act. Financial pressure, ideology, grievance, ego, coercion, or compromise by an external party. Rarely visible in telemetry; usually visible in human signals (HR concerns, expressed dissatisfaction, sudden lifestyle changes).</p>
  </div>
  <div class="matrix-phase">
    <span class="matrix-phase__num">02</span>
    <h4>🛠️ Means</h4>
    <p>What access and capability the insider already has. The matrix catalogs the techniques an insider can use without ever needing to "hack in" — they are already inside.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="matrix-phase">
    <span class="matrix-phase__num">03</span>
    <h4>🧭 Preparation</h4>
    <p>The steps an insider takes before the harmful action: reconnaissance of internal systems, acquisition of additional credentials, learning what the SOC watches. These are the early signals if anyone is looking.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="matrix-phase">
    <span class="matrix-phase__num">04</span>
    <h4>⚠️ Infringement</h4>
    <p>The actual harmful action. Theft of intellectual property, sabotage, fraud, exfiltration. The visible event most investigations start from.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="matrix-phase">
    <span class="matrix-phase__num">05</span>
    <h4>🧽 Anti-forensics</h4>
    <p>What the insider does to make the action hard to trace. Log clearing, timestomping, using accounts they should not (or accounts that "belong" to someone else). Cleanup is itself a signal.</p>
  </div>
</div>

# What insider analysis catches

<div class="insider-patterns">
  <div class="insider-pattern">
    <h4>📦 Slow-burn data collection</h4>
    <p>An insider gradually building a personal cache of sensitive documents over months. Each individual access looks legitimate; the trajectory is the signal.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="insider-pattern">
    <h4>🚪 Access creep before a known event</h4>
    <p>Requesting or accumulating permissions in the weeks before a known departure, performance review, or organizational change. Correlation with HR signals is what surfaces this.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="insider-pattern">
    <h4>🪪 Shared-account misuse</h4>
    <p>A shared account being used by an unexpected person. Hard to detect because the audit trail is intentionally ambiguous. Behavioral baselining of the shared account against its expected users is the strongest approach.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="insider-pattern">
    <h4>🔇 Anti-forensic cleanup</h4>
    <p>Log clearing, history wiping, USB usage from an account that has never used removable media. Each is a signal in its own right; the combination is almost always intentional.</p>
  </div>
</div>

# Mitigation strategies

- **User behavior analytics (UEBA)** for per-identity baselining. Already covered in the behavioral framework page.
- **Data loss prevention (DLP)** to detect bulk movement of sensitive data, especially to unmanaged destinations.
- **Privileged access management (PAM)** so admin actions are channeled through a system that records them rather than performed directly with admin credentials.
- **Just-in-time access** so privileges expire after the time they were needed, rather than lingering as standing access.
- **Coordinated escalation paths** with HR, legal, and counsel. Insider investigations have implications no other phase of triage does.

<Callout variant="warn" title="Insider investigations need different escalation">
An insider investigation is one of the few in triage where the analyst should usually <em>not</em> directly engage the subject. Coordinate with HR and legal before the subject becomes aware they are being investigated. Premature exposure can compromise both the security investigation and any subsequent legal action.
</Callout>

<KeyTakeaway>
Insider analysis is the fourth pillar of Subject and the one most likely to involve people outside the SOC. The methodology applies the same four dimensions but with adjusted expectations and a different escalation path. Start with risk. Escalate to threat only with evidence.
</KeyTakeaway>
