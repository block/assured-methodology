## The handoff contract

<dl class="handoff-contract">
  <div class="handoff-contract__row">
    <dt>Deliverable</dt>
    <dd>The Subject handoff: the <a href="#the-handoff-itself">seven sections below</a>, under one page. Most of it is structured selection from Subject's notes, not new writing. The chapter's four products map onto it: the entity map is section 2, baselines and deviations with their confidence are section 3, the relationship graph is section 2's classification rings plus the lateral-movement candidates in section 5, and insider indicators are section 6 (insider posture).   </div>
  <div class="handoff-contract__row">
    <dt>Close path</dt>
    <dd>Subject closes nothing on its own. A clean verdict on all four dimensions is recorded explicitly and handed to Scope as a narrow recommendation; the close itself happens at the Escalation decision (Case B in the <a href="/docs/subject/example/#case-b">working example</a>).</dd>
  </div>
  <div class="handoff-contract__row">
    <dt>What Scope asks</dt>
    <dd><em>Where are the lines?</em> A window per entity, the environmental boundary, the users in scope, and the regulatory flags. Subject's recommendation is the first draft of each.</dd>
  </div>
  <div class="handoff-contract__row">
    <dt>Depth</dt>
    <dd>At Level 1, Subject and Scope run as one sweep and the handoff is a few lines in the <a href="/docs/documentation/templates/">alert report</a>. At Level 2, write all seven sections. A clean Subject never moves a case back down a level.</dd>
  </div>
  <div class="handoff-contract__row">
    <dt>You are ready if</dt>
    <dd>
      <ul>
        <li>Every entity examined is on the map, cleared ones included, and every verdict carries a confidence label. A silent gap is not a verdict.</li>
        <li>Insider posture and regulatory flags are recorded, even when the entry is "none observed".</li>
      </ul>
    </dd>
  </div>
</dl>

<Callout variant="warn" title="Live compromise does not wait for the handoff">
If Subject confirms a canonical escalation criterion at High confidence (a hijacked session still valid, an active persistence artifact, data leaving), the <a href="/docs/escalation/criteria/#tripwire">escalation tripwire</a> fires from Subject: page the IR lead, <a href="/docs/escalation/preserve/">preserve first</a>, and run only the reversible pre-authorized actions. The handoff becomes scoping support under IR's clock. Suspicion is different: an unconfirmed signal refines Scope. Case C in the <a href="/docs/subject/example/#case-c">working example</a> shows the pattern.
</Callout>

## The handoff itself

1. **Investigation header:** case and alert IDs, analyst, timestamp, a two-sentence summary, and whether the tripwire has fired (and who owns the case).
2. **<DefineTerm term="Entity" /> inventory:** one row per identity examined, with type (user, service account, host or device, application or OAuth grant, session or token, resource, artifact, network identifier, threat), classification (primary, secondary, downstream, originating threat) and a per-dimension verdict, which may read "examined and cleared". A non-human identity's row names the person or pipeline behind it ([who is behind this](/docs/subject/non-human-identities/#answering-who-is-behind-this)).
3. **Confidence summary:** for each implicated entity, the verdict, the baseline behind it and a label on the [shared rubric](/docs/documentation/standards/#confidence).
4. **Open questions:** each one an explicit Uncover target.
5. **Recommended scope parameters:** a window per entity anchored to its last known-good point (for a non-human identity, its [last credential rotation](/docs/subject/non-human-identities/#answering-who-is-behind-this)), the environmental boundary (including what is out), and the lateral-movement candidates.
6. **Insider posture:** activity mapped to the <DefineTerm term="Insider Threat Matrix" display="Insider Threat Matrix" />, or "none observed". Personal context stays with the insider-program owner, HR and legal; it never enters the SOC handoff.
7. **Regulatory flags:** Alert's flag as written (reached, or only reachable), the data classes in reach, and the timestamp of reasonable cause. Some clocks start at awareness, not confirmation; Scope's [regulatory check](/docs/scope/regulatory/) starts here.

The common failures are silent ones: a secondary identity that never reaches the map, a verdict with no confidence label, adjacent entities left out without a word, and a missing insider-posture line.

<KeyTakeaway>
Subject produces the map; Scope draws the lines. The handoff is seven sections of structured selection, scaled to the depth level, and a clean verdict is recorded as carefully as a dirty one. When Subject confirms an escalation criterion, the handoff becomes scoping support under IR's clock instead of a gate.
</KeyTakeaway>
