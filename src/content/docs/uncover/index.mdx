With Alert, Subject, and Scope established, this phase pulls <DefineTerm term="Telemetry" display="telemetry" />, correlates it, identifies anomalies, and reconstructs the chain of activity from first action to last. <Callout variant="info" title="What Uncover is for">
Alert, Subject, and Scope told the analyst what fired, who was involved, and where the lines are. Uncover is where the analyst actually answers the question: what happened, who or what did it, and what did it touch, including the real possibility that the activity is legitimate. Uncover ends with a narrative the analyst can defend.

The boundary with Subject is worth stating plainly. Subject runs bounded, entity-centric lookups (identity, privilege, recent authentication, baseline) to answer who acted and whether it is normal for them. Uncover runs activity-centric, multi-source queries to reconstruct what happened. If the query is about the entity, you are in Subject; if it is about the activity chain, you are in Uncover.
</Callout>

### When Uncover is done

Uncover is finished when the case file holds the six things Risk needs, described on the <a href="/docs/uncover/transition/">Uncover-to-Risk transition</a>:

- **The evidence chain:** what happened, in order, with timestamps, each step tied to the source that showed it and mapped to ATT&CK where it applies.
- **Entity verdicts:** which of Scope's entities were involved and which were investigated and ruled out, each with its evidence.
- **Intelligence context:** which intel matched, at what confidence, and whether it stayed a hypothesis or was borne out by your own telemetry.
- **Coverage gaps:** what could not be examined (missing telemetry, expired retention, blind sensors) and why.
- **Detection gaps:** techniques observed but not flagged automatically, <a href="/docs/detection-feedback/">written so detection engineering can act on them</a>.
- **The hypothesis ledger:** every hypothesis with its status, refuted ones included, plus the dry-thread checks for any thread closed on empty results.

## Why Uncover deserves its own phase

- **Evidence is plural:** <DefineTerm term="Endpoint" /> telemetry alone is rarely enough, and network logs alone are rarely enough. Uncover is the phase that brings sources together, so the analyst can answer a question the way only correlated data can.
- **ATT&CK is the vocabulary:** mapping findings to <DefineTerm term="MITRE ATT&CK" /> turns "the attacker did this and then this" into "T1566.001 → T1059.001 → T1071.001". That precision matters at handoff and in trend analysis. For a non-technical audience, translate it back into tactic names (Initial Access → Execution → Command and Control).
- **Intel is a hypothesis source:** <DefineTerm term="Threat Intelligence" display="threat intelligence" /> is most useful as a hypothesis generator, not as ground truth. Uncover uses intel to decide what to look for, then validates it against the evidence in the environment.
- **Tools serve the question:** a common Uncover failure is starting with a tool ("let me run a <DefineTerm term="SIEM (concept)" display="SIEM" /> query") instead of a question. The method asks what the analyst needs to know, then chooses the tool that can answer it.
