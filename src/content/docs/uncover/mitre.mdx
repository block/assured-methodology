## Three matrices, four components

<div class="att-grid">
  <div class="att-card">
    <h4>🏢 Enterprise</h4>
    <p>Techniques targeting corporate environments across Windows, macOS, Linux, cloud services, and network infrastructure. The most widely used matrix.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="att-card">
    <h4>📱 Mobile</h4>
    <p>Specialized framework documenting threats against Android and iOS mobile ecosystems.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="att-card">
    <h4>⚙️ ICS</h4>
    <p>Dedicated matrix for industrial control systems and operational technology environments with unique safety and availability challenges.</p>
  </div>
</div>

### The four levels

<div class="mitre-grid">
  <div class="mitre-card">
    <span class="mitre-card__tag">Tactics</span>
    <h3>The why</h3>
    <p>The adversary's strategic objectives. Categories representing the "why" behind each phase of an attack. Things like Initial Access, Persistence, Defense Evasion, Impact.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="mitre-card">
    <span class="mitre-card__tag">Techniques</span>
    <h3>The how</h3>
    <p>The specific method used to accomplish a tactic. Each technique has a unique ID (e.g., T1566 for Phishing). Granular enough to map to detection rules and to teach analysts what to look for.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="mitre-card">
    <span class="mitre-card__tag">Sub-techniques</span>
    <h3>The how, narrower</h3>
    <p>Granular variations of a parent technique with distinct characteristics. T1566 (Phishing) has sub-techniques T1566.001 (Attachment), T1566.002 (Link), T1566.003 (via Service), T1566.004 (Voice).</p>
  </div>
  <div class="mitre-card">
    <span class="mitre-card__tag">Procedures</span>
    <h3>The exact thing</h3>
    <p>Documented examples of techniques as implemented by specific threat actors in observed attacks. The granular operational steps that distinguish one actor from another.</p>
  </div>
</div>

<Callout variant="info" title="ATT&CK as community knowledge">
The framework's strength is community-driven evolution. Rather than relying on theoretical attack models, ATT&CK draws from documented incidents, <DefineTerm term="Threat" display="threat" />-intel reporting, and direct contributions from security practitioners worldwide. The framework stays current and actionable as adversaries develop new techniques and repurpose existing ones.
</Callout>

<Callout variant="info" title="Looking up a technique by ID">
Every ATT&CK identifier resolves to a canonical page at <a href="https://attack.mitre.org/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">attack.mitre.org</a>. The URL pattern is predictable: <code>https://attack.mitre.org/techniques/T1059/</code> for a top-level technique, <code>https://attack.mitre.org/techniques/T1566/001/</code> for a sub-technique, and <code>https://attack.mitre.org/tactics/TA0001/</code> for a tactic. When this site cites an ID, you can paste it into that URL pattern to reach the authoritative description, detection guidance, and known procedures.
</Callout>

## The 14 tactics

Enterprise ATT&CK defines 14 tactics: the strategic objectives an adversary pursues during an intrusion. The twelve below are the ones you can observe in your own telemetry; the callout after the grid covers the two pre-intrusion tactics. Adversaries rarely progress linearly through them, and they may revisit phases multiple times during a single campaign. Identifying which tactic you are observing is what tells you which countermeasures apply.

<div class="tactics-grid">
  <div class="tactic-card">
    <span class="tactic-card__id">TA0001</span>
    <h4>Initial access</h4>
    <p>Techniques to gain entry. Exploiting external apps, phishing, leveraging trusted relationships.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="tactic-card">
    <span class="tactic-card__id">TA0002</span>
    <h4>Execution</h4>
    <p>Running adversary-controlled code. Command lines, PowerShell, malicious macros.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="tactic-card">
    <span class="tactic-card__id">TA0003</span>
    <h4>Persistence</h4>
    <p>Maintaining access across restarts, credentials, or interruptions. Scheduled tasks, startup items, backdoors.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="tactic-card">
    <span class="tactic-card__id">TA0004</span>
    <h4>Privilege escalation</h4>
    <p>Getting higher-level permissions. Vulnerability exploits, token manipulation, UAC bypass.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="tactic-card">
    <span class="tactic-card__id">TA0005</span>
    <h4>Defense evasion</h4>
    <p>Avoiding detection. Disabling tools, obfuscating files, leveraging legitimate credentials.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="tactic-card">
    <span class="tactic-card__id">TA0006</span>
    <h4>Credential access</h4>
    <p>Stealing account names and passwords. Keylogging, credential dumping, brute force.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="tactic-card">
    <span class="tactic-card__id">TA0007</span>
    <h4>Discovery</h4>
    <p>Learning about the environment. Network scanning, account enumeration, system info gathering.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="tactic-card">
    <span class="tactic-card__id">TA0008</span>
    <h4>Lateral movement</h4>
    <p>Moving through the environment. Remote services, internal spearphishing, pass-the-hash.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="tactic-card">
    <span class="tactic-card__id">TA0009</span>
    <h4>Collection</h4>
    <p>Gathering data of interest. Screen capture, local system data, browser data.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="tactic-card">
    <span class="tactic-card__id">TA0011</span>
    <h4>Command and control</h4>
    <p>Communicating with controlled systems. Encrypted channels, web services, custom protocols.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="tactic-card">
    <span class="tactic-card__id">TA0010</span>
    <h4>Exfiltration</h4>
    <p>Stealing data. Alternative protocols, scheduled transfers, physical removal.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="tactic-card">
    <span class="tactic-card__id">TA0040</span>
    <h4>Impact</h4>
    <p>Disrupting availability or integrity. Ransomware encryption, defacement, data destruction.</p>
  </div>
</div>

<Callout variant="info" title="Two pre-intrusion tactics rarely observable from inside">
ATT&CK also defines <strong>TA0043 Reconnaissance</strong> (attacker information-gathering before initial access) and <strong>TA0042 Resource Development</strong> (infrastructure and capability setup, domain registration, malware compilation, account creation). Both happen *before* the attacker touches your environment. They are useful context for attribution and threat-intel correlation, but you will rarely catch them in your own telemetry during triage, they show up in your logs as the *consequences* (the registered domain that resolves to a C2 IP, the spear-phish that lands in a mailbox), not the activity itself. Read them when you're consuming a threat-intel report; don't expect to detect them on shift.
</Callout>

## Procedures: how specific actors implement techniques

Procedures represent the specific implementations of techniques used by threat actors or <DefineTerm term="Malware" display="malware" /> families. While multiple adversaries may employ identical techniques, their procedural implementations often differ significantly based on sophistication, resources, and intent.

<div class="proc-grid">
  <div class="proc-card">
    <span class="proc-card__actor">APT29 (Cozy Bear)</span>
    <h4>T1566.001, Spearphishing Attachment</h4>
    <p>Uses legitimate cloud storage services to host malware. Sends targeted emails with links to documents containing malicious macros. Macros establish persistence using WMI event subscriptions and execute PowerShell commands for C2 communication.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="proc-card">
    <span class="proc-card__actor">FIN7</span>
    <h4>T1059.001, PowerShell</h4>
    <p>Deploys heavily obfuscated PowerShell scripts via phishing attachments. Multiple layers of encoding evade detection. Scripts create scheduled tasks for persistence and inject Carbanak malware directly into memory.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="proc-card">
    <span class="proc-card__actor">Lazarus Group</span>
    <h4>T1068, Exploitation for Privilege Escalation</h4>
    <p>Exploits Windows privilege-escalation vulnerabilities using custom exploit code. Combines multiple vulnerabilities in sequence and employs anti-forensic techniques to hide exploitation artifacts.</p>
  </div>
</div>

<Reveal summary="Why procedural variation matters" variant="info">

Two actors might both leverage PowerShell for <DefineTerm term="Execution" display="execution" /> (T1059.001) but with very different procedural choices.

One actor might deploy heavily obfuscated scripts with encrypted payloads, prioritizing stealth at the cost of speed. Another might use simpler one-liners that finish quickly but show plainly in logs.

Procedural patterns are what link an investigation to a known <DefineTerm term="Threat Actor" display="threat actor" /> when multiple groups use the same technique. They are also what tell defenders which detection rule is going to fire and which is going to miss.

</Reveal>

## How an analyst uses ATT&CK during Uncover

<div class="usage-grid">
  <div class="usage-card">
    <h4>📌 During Uncover</h4>
    <p>Map each observed behavior to its ATT&CK technique. The chain of techniques becomes the investigation's narrative spine.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="usage-card">
    <h4>🤝 For handoff</h4>
    <p>Naming techniques lets the IR team start work from a shared model rather than re-reading the analyst's notes.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="usage-card">
    <h4>🔧 For detection engineering</h4>
    <p>When a technique was observed but not detected automatically, that gap is the feature request for a new rule.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="usage-card">
    <h4>📈 For trend analysis</h4>
    <p>Tagging cases with techniques over time shows what the SOC sees most often, which feeds prioritization.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="usage-card">
    <h4>📊 For executive communication</h4>
    <p>ATT&CK gives technical teams a vocabulary executive leadership recognizes after one orientation. Bridges tactical and strategic conversations.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="usage-card">
    <h4>🎯 For gap analysis</h4>
    <p>Mapping defensive coverage against the matrix surfaces where detection is strong and where it is thin. Drives investment decisions.</p>
  </div>
</div>

<KeyTakeaway>
ATT&CK is the vocabulary that makes Uncover findings portable. The chain of techniques is the artifact that survives the analyst and the alert; "T1566.001 → T1059.001 → T1071.001" travels better than free-form prose, and it travels into IR, detection engineering, threat trends, and executive briefings without translation.
</KeyTakeaway>
