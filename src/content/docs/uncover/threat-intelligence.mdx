# Five ways to use intel

<div class="ti-grid">
  <div class="ti-card"><h4>🎯 Indicator matching</h4><p>The simplest use. Compare observed artifacts (hashes, domains, IPs) against known-bad lists. Useful but limited because adversaries change indicators routinely.</p></div>
  <div class="ti-card"><h4>👤 Adversary attribution</h4><p>Map observed behavior to a named threat actor or campaign. Useful for context and reporting, less directly actionable.</p></div>
  <div class="ti-card"><h4>🔍 TTP-based hunting</h4><p>Use known adversary techniques to search for similar patterns. Far more durable than indicator matching because techniques change less often than indicators.</p></div>
  <div class="ti-card"><h4>⚙️ Automated enrichment</h4><p>Append intel data to alerts in flight. Domain age, reputation, geolocation, campaign tags. Reduces the analyst's manual lookup work.</p></div>
  <div class="ti-card"><h4>🕸️ Dark-web monitoring</h4><p>Watch for leaked credentials, organization mentions, or attack planning. Highly variable signal-to-noise but valuable for early warning.</p></div>
</div>

# Threat intelligence tiers

Intelligence comes in four tiers, each useful for different audiences and uses.

<div class="tier-list">
  <div class="tier"><span class="tier__label">Strategic</span><p>High-level trends, geopolitical context, industry-wide threat patterns. For leadership and risk planning.</p></div>
  <div class="tier"><span class="tier__label">Operational</span><p>Campaigns, threat actor objectives, near-term targeting. For SOC managers and threat-hunt program leads.</p></div>
  <div class="tier"><span class="tier__label">Tactical</span><p>TTPs, attack patterns, defensive playbooks. For detection engineers and senior analysts.</p></div>
  <div class="tier"><span class="tier__label">Technical</span><p>IoCs: hashes, domains, IPs. For automated detection systems and triage analysts.</p></div>
</div>

# Validation and scoring

Not all intel is equal. Before acting on a piece of intelligence, the analyst asks:

- **Source reliability.** Where did this come from? Is the source a trusted commercial feed, an open-source community, or a single unverified post?
- **Recency.** When was this observed? Indicators age out fast.
- **Specificity.** Is the intel about the exact thing being investigated, or a loose analogy?
- **Corroboration.** Does any other source say the same thing?

<Callout variant="info" title="Intel is a hypothesis, not a verdict">
Threat intelligence tells the analyst what to look for. It does not tell them what they have. Validate intel-driven hypotheses against the actual telemetry before acting.
</Callout>

<KeyTakeaway>
Threat intelligence makes Uncover faster when used as a hypothesis generator. It makes Uncover slower when treated as ground truth. The methodology asks the analyst to use intel deliberately, score it, and confirm with the environment's own evidence.
</KeyTakeaway>
