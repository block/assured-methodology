## Five ways to use intel

<div class="ti-grid">
  <a href="#indicator-matching" class="ti-card"><h4>🎯 Indicator matching</h4><p>The simplest use. Compare observed artifacts against known-bad lists. Reactive by nature.</p></a>
  <a href="#attribution" class="ti-card"><h4>👤 Adversary attribution</h4><p>Connect observed behavior to specific actors or campaigns. Useful for context, less directly actionable.</p></a>
  <a href="#ttp-hunting" class="ti-card"><h4>🔍 TTP-based hunting</h4><p>Use known techniques to search for similar patterns. More durable than indicator matching.</p></a>
  <a href="#enrichment" class="ti-card"><h4>⚙️ Automated enrichment</h4><p>Append intel data to alerts in flight. Reduces manual lookup work and speeds triage.</p></a>
  <a href="#dark-web" class="ti-card"><h4>🕸️ Dark-web monitoring</h4><p>Watch for leaked credentials, organization mentions, attack planning. Early warning signal.</p></a>
</div>

<Callout variant="info" title="The integration principle">
<DefineTerm term="Threat Intelligence" display="Threat intelligence" /> should be used not in isolation, but as a correlation layer applied across internal data sets such as logs, EDR <DefineTerm term="Telemetry" display="telemetry" />, and <DefineTerm term="Network Traffic" display="network traffic" />. Its goals during Uncover are validation, prioritization, contextualization, and prediction.
</Callout>

<h2 id="indicator-matching">Indicator matching</h2>

Indicator matching compares local telemetry (DNS queries, process <DefineTerm term="Hashes" display="hashes" />, outbound connections) against known IOCs from <DefineTerm term="Threat" display="threat" />-intel sources. Sources span commercial threat feeds, government advisories, ISACs, open-source databases, and internal reports.

<div class="ioc-grid">
  <div class="ioc-card">
    <h4>🌐 Network indicators</h4>
    <p>IP addresses, URLs, and domain names associated with malicious infrastructure (C2 servers, phishing sites, malware staging).</p>
  </div>
  <div class="ioc-card">
    <h4>📄 File indicators</h4>
    <p>Cryptographic hashes (MD5/SHA256) identifying known malware payloads or tools.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="ioc-card">
    <h4>📧 Email indicators</h4>
    <p>Suspicious senders, subject patterns, attachment signatures from known campaigns.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="ioc-card">
    <h4>🧬 Behavioral indicators</h4>
    <p>Specific system modifications: registry changes, scheduled tasks, mutex creation, or other distinctive patterns observed in known malware.</p>
  </div>
</div>

### Indicator matching process

The six steps below are the pipeline that stands up and maintains indicator matching, and most of it is CTI-team and detection-engineering work, not triage. The on-shift analyst lives at steps 04 and 05, querying newly published indicators against history and receiving the real-time matches as alerts, and contributes to step 06 every time a close records whether a match was real. The rest is here so the analyst knows what the matches they consume rest on.

<div class="ti-process">
  <div class="ti-process__step">
    <span class="ti-process__num">01</span>
    <div>
      <h4>Ingestion</h4>
      <p>Collect and normalize indicators from diverse sources into structured formats optimized for automated matching.</p>
    </div>
  </div>
  <div class="ti-process__step">
    <span class="ti-process__num">02</span>
    <div>
      <h4>Validation</h4>
      <p>Evaluate indicator quality, relevance, and reliability before operational deployment.</p>
    </div>
  </div>
  <div class="ti-process__step">
    <span class="ti-process__num">03</span>
    <div>
      <h4>Integration</h4>
      <p>Deploy matching capabilities across SIEM, EDR, NDR, and proxy systems.</p>
    </div>
  </div>
  <div class="ti-process__step">
    <span class="ti-process__num">04</span>
    <div>
      <h4>Retroactive hunting</h4>
      <p>Analyze historical data to uncover previously undetected compromises using newly acquired indicators.</p>
    </div>
  </div>
  <div class="ti-process__step">
    <span class="ti-process__num">05</span>
    <div>
      <h4>Real-time monitoring</h4>
      <p>Implement alerting mechanisms for immediate detection of indicator matches in ongoing activity.</p>
    </div>
  </div>
  <div class="ti-process__step">
    <span class="ti-process__num">06</span>
    <div>
      <h4>Feedback</h4>
      <p>Systematically document true and false-positives to continuously refine intelligence quality.</p>
    </div>
  </div>
</div>

<Callout variant="warn" title="The limits of indicator matching">
Indicator matching is fundamentally reactive and depends on current, verified <DefineTerm term="Intelligence" display="intelligence" />. Advanced adversaries deliberately employ techniques like <DefineTerm term="Domain" display="domain" /> rotation, <DefineTerm term="Fileless Malware" display="fileless malware" />, and living-off-the-land tactics to circumvent <DefineTerm term="Signature-Based Detection" display="signature-based detection" />. Indicator matching is necessary; it is not sufficient. Pair it with behavioral analysis and TTP-based hunting for defensive depth.
</Callout>

<h2 id="attribution">Adversary attribution</h2>

Adversary attribution is the methodical process of connecting observed <DefineTerm term="Attack Patterns" display="attack patterns" /> to specific threat actors, groups, or campaigns. The analysis integrates behavioral signatures, <DefineTerm term="Infrastructure" display="infrastructure" /> reuse, <DefineTerm term="Malware" display="malware" /> family characteristics, and documented TTPs from authoritative threat-intel sources such as Mandiant, CrowdStrike, <DefineTerm term="Recorded Future" />, and the MITRE ATT&amp;CK framework.

<div class="attribution-grid">
  <div class="attribution-card attribution-card--tech">
    <h4>Technical evidence</h4>
    <p>Malware code signatures, infrastructure overlap, compilation artifacts. Foundational linkages to known threat actors.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="attribution-card attribution-card--behav">
    <h4>Behavioral patterns</h4>
    <p>Victim selection criteria, operational timeframes, distinctive tactical approaches. Correlation points beyond the binary.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="attribution-card attribution-card--strat">
    <h4>Strategic context</h4>
    <p>Geopolitical developments, threat-actor motivations, industry targeting preferences. The "why" that anchors attribution.</p>
  </div>
</div>

### Confidence levels

Attribution claims use the same LOW / MEDIUM / HIGH scale as every other claim in the case file (the <a href="/docs/documentation/standards/#confidence">shared rubric</a>). The grid below translates each level into attribution-specific evidence, so the analyst knows what an attribution claim must rest on before it earns its label.

<div class="confidence-grid">
  <div class="confidence-card confidence-card--low">
    <span class="confidence-card__tag">LOW</span>
    <p>Isolated technical indicators with significant potential for coincidental overlap. May suggest investigative directions but requires substantial corroborating evidence before operational use.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="confidence-card confidence-card--med">
    <span class="confidence-card__tag">MEDIUM</span>
    <p>Multiple correlated indicators across diverse categories (tooling, infrastructure, TTPs) that collectively point toward a specific threat actor or group.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="confidence-card confidence-card--high">
    <span class="confidence-card__tag">HIGH</span>
    <p>Comprehensive evidence portfolio including unique identifiers, distinctive operational patterns, and multiple reinforcing data points indicating a specific actor with minimal alternative explanations.</p>
  </div>
</div>

<Reveal summary="Attribution challenges to recognize" variant="warn">

- **False flag operations.** Sophisticated adversaries deliberately impersonate other threat actors to obscure responsibility and complicate attribution.
- **Shared tooling.** Widespread use of common malware families or publicly available frameworks creates attribution ambiguity.
- **Evolution of TTPs.** Actors change their techniques over time, so a profile built on last year's tradecraft can misattribute this year's intrusion.
- **Confirmation bias.** Analytical tendency to disproportionately value evidence supporting initial attribution hypotheses while discounting contradictory indicators.

Attribution at low or medium confidence should be labeled as such in the case file. Promoting low-confidence attribution to operational decisions is the most common attribution failure mode.

</Reveal>

<h2 id="ttp-hunting">Threat hunting with TTPs</h2>

Proactive <DefineTerm term="Threat Hunting" display="threat hunting" /> based on known Tactics, Techniques, and Procedures uncovers sophisticated threats that have evaded traditional detection. Unlike IOC matching, which relies on known <DefineTerm term="Artifacts" display="artifacts" />, TTP-based hunting focuses on adversary behavior patterns regardless of the particular tools employed.

By transforming TTPs into actionable hunt hypotheses (for example, "Identify <DefineTerm term="PowerShell" /> <DefineTerm term="Execution" display="execution" /> originating from non-administrative accounts" or "Detect scheduled tasks created within temporary directories") security teams can methodically query logs, EDR telemetry, or SIEM platforms for evidence of stealthy or emerging attacks.

<div class="hunt-grid">
  <div class="hunt-card">
    <span class="hunt-card__num">01</span>
    <h4>Hypothesis formation</h4>
    <p>Develop specific, testable theories based on threat intelligence about adversary TTPs relevant to your environment.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="hunt-card">
    <span class="hunt-card__num">02</span>
    <h4>Query development</h4>
    <p>Create data queries or analytics that would identify evidence of the targeted behavior across relevant data sources.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="hunt-card">
    <span class="hunt-card__num">03</span>
    <h4>Hunt execution</h4>
    <p>Systematically search through current and historical data for patterns matching the hypothesis.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="hunt-card">
    <span class="hunt-card__num">04</span>
    <h4>Finding analysis</h4>
    <p>Investigate initial results to eliminate false-positives and validate genuine security concerns.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="hunt-card">
    <span class="hunt-card__num">05</span>
    <h4>Process improvement</h4>
    <p>Document findings, refine queries, and implement persistent detection for discovered techniques.</p>
  </div>
</div>

<Callout variant="info" title="Why TTPs outlast indicators">
TTP-based hunting keeps working because behavior is expensive to change. An attacker can recompile malware or stand up new infrastructure in an afternoon, but abandoning a technique that works means retooling an entire operation. The techniques stay stable long after every indicator has rotated.
</Callout>

<h2 id="enrichment">Automated threat enrichment</h2>

Automated enrichment attaches threat-intelligence context to events as they arrive, so the analyst opens an alert that already carries reputation, attribution, and infrastructure detail instead of spending the first minutes of triage on manual lookups.

<div class="enrich-cols">
  <div class="enrich-col">
    <h4>Enrichment data types</h4>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>Reputation scores.</strong> Trust ratings for IPs, domains, files, URLs.</li>
      <li><strong>Threat-actor attribution.</strong> Adversary groups associated with observed indicators.</li>
      <li><strong>Malware classification.</strong> Family, variant, and capability profiles.</li>
      <li><strong>Contextual metadata.</strong> WHOIS, infrastructure details, certificate validation.</li>
      <li><strong>Historical intelligence.</strong> Previous indicator sightings and campaign activities.</li>
    </ul>
  </div>
  <div class="enrich-col">
    <h4>Common enrichment services</h4>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>VirusTotal.</strong> Multi-engine file and URL reputation.</li>
      <li><strong>Shodan / Censys.</strong> Internet-exposed services and vulnerability intel.</li>
      <li><strong>GreyNoise.</strong> Background-noise filtering and benign-activity identification.</li>
      <li><strong>Microsoft Defender Threat Intelligence (MDTI, formerly RiskIQ / PassiveTotal).</strong> Domain and IP infrastructure mapping.</li>
      <li><strong>AlienVault OTX.</strong> Crowd-sourced threat indicators and community intel.</li>
    </ul>
  </div>
  <div class="enrich-col">
    <h4>Implementation approaches</h4>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>Native tool integration.</strong> Purpose-built connectors within SIEM/SOAR.</li>
      <li><strong>API orchestration.</strong> Custom scripts or middleware for intelligence aggregation.</li>
      <li><strong>Intelligence platforms.</strong> Dedicated TIP solutions with advanced enrichment.</li>
      <li><strong>Inline enrichment.</strong> Network tools that tag traffic with real-time threat context.</li>
    </ul>
  </div>
</div>

<h2 id="dark-web">Dark web and underground monitoring</h2>

Dark web surveillance can detect exposed credentials, breach preparations, targeted attack discussions, <DefineTerm term="Exploit" display="exploit" /> development, and brand impersonation. This is a program capability, not a triage activity: specialized vendors and dedicated threat-intel teams do the monitoring, using automated scraping plus human intelligence, and the collection challenges below are theirs to solve. What reaches the triage analyst is the output, a credential-exposure alert in the queue or an enrichment note on a case, and the reason to understand the collection is to weigh that output: where it came from, how noisy the space is, and why it arrives with a confidence label rather than as fact.

<div class="darkweb-grid">
  <div class="darkweb-card">
    <h4>Sources</h4>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>Criminal forums.</strong> Boards for technique sharing, service sales, recruiting.</li>
      <li><strong>Marketplaces.</strong> Underground markets for malware, exploits, credentials, stolen data.</li>
      <li><strong>Leak sites.</strong> Where ransomware groups and hacktivists publish stolen data.</li>
      <li><strong>Chat platforms.</strong> Encrypted messaging for private actor communications.</li>
      <li><strong>Paste sites.</strong> Anonymous data dumps and credential leaks.</li>
    </ul>
  </div>
  <div class="darkweb-card">
    <h4>Intelligence value</h4>
    <ul>
      <li>Pre-attack indicators before traditional detection fires.</li>
      <li>Visibility into adversary capabilities and intentions.</li>
      <li>Advance warning of emerging attack techniques.</li>
      <li>Insight into targeting priorities of threat actors.</li>
    </ul>
  </div>
  <div class="darkweb-card">
    <h4>Collection challenges</h4>
    <ul>
      <li>Access barriers to closed communities.</li>
      <li>Language and cultural context requirements.</li>
      <li>Signal-to-noise ratio in unstructured data.</li>
      <li>Legal and ethical considerations in collection.</li>
    </ul>
  </div>
  <div class="darkweb-card">
    <h4>Use cases</h4>
    <ul>
      <li>Credential exposure monitoring and forced resets.</li>
      <li>Early vulnerability patching based on exploit chatter.</li>
      <li>Brand protection and impersonation detection.</li>
      <li>Insider threat identification via data leakage.</li>
    </ul>
  </div>
</div>

## Threat intelligence tiers

Intelligence operates across three tiers, each serving distinct purposes and distinct audiences. Triage runs almost entirely on the tactical tier; the operational tier serves threat hunters and detection engineers between cases; the strategic tier is program material for leadership and architects. The table is here so the analyst can place any piece of intel that crosses their desk, not because a triage case calls for a geopolitical briefing.

<div class="tier-table">
  <div class="tier-table__row tier-table__head">
    <div></div>
    <div>Tactical</div>
    <div>Operational</div>
    <div>Strategic</div>
  </div>
  <div class="tier-table__row">
    <div class="tier-table__label">Time horizon</div>
    <div>Hours to days</div>
    <div>Weeks to months</div>
    <div>Months to years</div>
  </div>
  <div class="tier-table__row">
    <div class="tier-table__label">Primary users</div>
    <div>SOC analysts, IR responders</div>
    <div>Threat hunters, detection eng.</div>
    <div>Leadership, architects</div>
  </div>
  <div class="tier-table__row">
    <div class="tier-table__label">Format</div>
    <div>Machine-readable indicators</div>
    <div>Behavioral analytics, TTP docs</div>
    <div>Reports, briefings, risk assessments</div>
  </div>
  <div class="tier-table__row">
    <div class="tier-table__label">Measurement</div>
    <div>Detection effectiveness, FP rate</div>
    <div>Campaign coverage, TTP detection</div>
    <div>Risk reduction, strategic alignment</div>
  </div>
  <div class="tier-table__row">
    <div class="tier-table__label">Update frequency</div>
    <div>Continuous, automated</div>
    <div>Regular, semi-automated</div>
    <div>Periodic, manually curated</div>
  </div>
  <div class="tier-table__row">
    <div class="tier-table__label">Example sources</div>
    <div>OSINT feeds, TIP, vendor alerts</div>
    <div>Campaign analysis, malware research</div>
    <div>Industry reports, geopolitical analysis</div>
  </div>
</div>

<Callout variant="info" title="Why the tiers matter">
Tactical intel without operational context produces noise. Strategic intel without tactical depth produces strategy decks no one can operationalize. The methodology asks the SOC to use all three: tactical for triage, operational for hunting, strategic for the program-level decisions about what to detect and what to defend.
</Callout>

## Validation and confidence

Not all intel is equal. Before acting on a piece of intelligence, validate it across four checks.

<div class="validate-grid">
  <div class="validate-card">
    <h4>📡 Source reliability</h4>
    <p>Where did this come from? Premium sources include government agencies, established vendors, vetted ISACs. Higher-risk sources include crowdsourced platforms lacking verification. Triangulation across multiple providers strengthens confidence.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="validate-card">
    <h4>⏱️ Recency</h4>
    <p>When was this observed? Indicators age out fast. Systematically retire obsolete indicators (formerly malicious infrastructure repurposed as benign cloud edges, for instance).</p>
  </div>
  <div class="validate-card">
    <h4>🎯 Specificity</h4>
    <p>Is the intel about the exact thing being investigated, or a loose analogy? "This domain is associated with phishing" is less actionable than "This specific subdomain pattern is used by this specific campaign."</p>
  </div>
  <div class="validate-card">
    <h4>🔗 Corroboration</h4>
    <p>Does any other source say the same thing? Two independent sources reporting the same indicator is a stronger signal than one source repeating itself.</p>
  </div>
</div>

### One scale, not two

Validated intel is labeled with the same LOW / MEDIUM / HIGH scale used for <a href="#attribution">attribution</a> above and defined once in the <a href="/docs/documentation/standards/#confidence">shared rubric</a>: the four checks map directly onto it (corroboration and source reliability drive the level; recency and specificity cap it). The only state this section adds is **unverified**: intel that has not yet been scored against the four checks. Unverified intel can generate hypotheses, but it never drives an operational decision until it has been validated and labeled.

<Callout variant="info" title="Intel is a hypothesis, not a verdict">
Threat intelligence tells the analyst what to look for. It does not tell them what they have. Validate intel-driven hypotheses against the actual telemetry before acting. The matched IOC is the question, not the answer.
</Callout>

<KeyTakeaway>
Threat intelligence makes Uncover faster when used as a hypothesis generator. It makes Uncover slower when treated as ground truth. The methodology asks the analyst to use intel deliberately, score it across source/recency/specificity/corroboration, and confirm with the environment's own evidence before acting.
</KeyTakeaway>
